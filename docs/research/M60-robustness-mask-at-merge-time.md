## 0. Pre-registration

### 0.1 What is already known, and the one thing that is not

M28 measured this question once (facts 220–225) and found real signal. Its
result is **not** the result I need, for a reason that is structural rather than
a criticism:

| M28 measured | the merge actually does |
|---|---|
| disagreement between **all** frame pairs | averages only **gate-admitted** frames |
| whole-frame, unaligned, green sublattice, in Python | per-pixel, in the CFA domain, in the shipped Rust |
| a **detector**, with no merged image produced (its own §8) | must not be worse than one frame |

The merge does not average a stack. At each pixel it averages the frames whose
regularised focus response the calibrated noise model **cannot separate from the
winner's**. Motion changes the focus response. So the gate is already a
disagreement-sensitive device, and the open question — question 45, verbatim,
*"Does masking actually improve the merge?"* — turns on a quantity nobody has
measured: **disagreement among the frames the merge actually averages.**

That quantity can be small for either of two very different reasons, and this
milestone must not confuse them:

- **(i)** the corpus has no non-focus disagreement, or
- **(ii)** the gate already rejects it, so the residue inside the admitted set is
  noise.

Both would mean "do not build the mask", but they mean opposite things about
the *pipeline*, so §1 separates them.

I also record the prior on the other side, from question 45 itself: masking
removes contaminated samples **but also removes focus coverage**, which fact 213
measures as the scarcer resource in most brackets. The honest prior is that this
could go either way, and I am not predicting which.

### 0.2 The statistic

Per pixel `i`, over the set `A(i)` of frames the gate admitted (the winner is in
`A(i)` unconditionally), on green luma `L = (G1 + G2)/2` in raw DN:

```
n      = |A(i)|
mean   = (1/n) Σ L_f
s²     = (1/(n-1)) Σ (L_f - mean)²          unbiased sample variance
σ²pred = noise.green_luma_sigma(mean - black)²
chi2   = s² / σ²pred
```

**`E[chi2] = 1` for every `n ≥ 2` under the null "the admitted frames differ
only by sensor noise".** That null is *analytic*, not fitted — which is the
point. M28's detector needed a threshold calibrated on a clean stack; this one
does not, so the clean stack becomes a genuine test rather than the calibration
set. (Median is *not* used as the headline because `median(chi2)` depends on `n`
through `χ²(n-1)`; the mean does not.)

`chi2` is **undefined where `n = 1`**, and I expect that to be a large fraction
of pixels. This is deliberately the same shape as my own fact 688, where a
pre-registered statistic was the correct decider for the stated question and
simultaneously blind to the real defect because it was identically zero wherever
`n_eff = 1`. §1 therefore reports **exposure** — the fraction of pixels with
`n ≥ 2` — beside every `chi2`, and no `chi2` in this report is admissible
without its exposure.

**Discipline 358 disclosure, stated before measuring.** `chi2` divides by
`σ_pred` from the calibrated noise model. Therefore **no plant in this milestone
may be generated by scaling `σ_pred`** — that would make the statistic measure
its own arithmetic (facts 738, 740). Every plant here is built by moving **real
frame content**, following M28's design: a tile replaced by the same tile taken
`s` pixels away *in the same frame*, so noise, defocus, illumination and sensor
position are all correct by construction and nothing is synthesised. The
placebo is `s = 0`.

### 0.3 Stage 1 criteria — is there a problem?

Deciding runs: `109MSDCF` (22 frames; fact 180's quietest stack at 0.91% ptp, no
known motion), `coffee-bubbles` (19 frames, **constant focus** per fact 49,
bubbles drift and burst), `foam` (100 frames, rejected from the corpus because
the scene visibly evolves). Read-only; nothing is written under `D:\personal\macro`.

- **A1 — the placebo, made analytic.** On `109MSDCF`, mean `chi2` over pixels
  with `n ≥ 2` lands in **[0.80, 1.30]**.
  - *Met* → the normalisation is right, and any excess on the other stacks is
    real disagreement rather than a scaling error.
  - *Not met, high* → either the normalisation is wrong (the brief's reading) or
    the quietest stack in the corpus already carries non-noise disagreement.
    **I must distinguish these before building anything**, and A1b is how.
- **A1b — the photometric alternative, pre-registered as a rival explanation.**
  If A1 fails high, remove a single per-frame scalar gain, fitted as the **mean
  over all pixels** (fact 225's prescription, and *not* a median over a
  subject-centred crop, which fact 225 measures as the estimator that absorbs
  focus signal into gain). If mean `chi2` then falls by **≥ 50%** of its excess
  over 1.0, the disagreement is **photometric, not motion** — consistent with
  M31-V's refutation of fact 220's mechanism — and the correct repair is §8
  item 5's per-frame photometric normalisation, **not** a robustness mask. That
  is a complete result and I stop there.
- **A2 — ordering, registered from what the stacks are, not from the numbers.**
  Mean `chi2` must satisfy `109MSDCF < coffee-bubbles` and
  `109MSDCF < foam`, with the larger of the two motion stacks exceeding
  `109MSDCF` by **≥ 1.5×**. If the ordering fails, the statistic is measuring
  something other than scene instability and no conclusion may be drawn from it.
- **A3 — the decider for whether to build at all.** Let `reach` be the fraction
  of **all** pixels that are both `n ≥ 2` and above `109MSDCF`'s 99th percentile
  of `chi2`. If `reach < 1.0%` on **every** stack measured, then whatever
  disagreement exists is not inside the set the merge averages, the gate is
  already doing the rejection, and **the mask is machinery for a problem the
  merge does not have.** I report that and stop, per the brief.

### 0.4 Stage 2 criteria — only if Stage 1 says build

- **B1 — the governing invariant, as a proof rather than a measurement.** The
  merge must never be worse than a single frame. Today that is *structural*:
  `Estimator::fold` admits the winner unconditionally, so `n_eff ≥ 1`. A
  robustness mask can break it by down-weighting good frames in exactly the
  low-texture regions where disagreement is hardest to tell from noise. I
  pre-register the strong form: **the mask must be structurally incapable of
  producing an estimate with a higher predicted variance than the winner alone**
  — the winner's weight is never reduced, and total weight never falls below 1.
  This is to be asserted in code and exercised by a test, not established by
  averaging over a stack.
- **B2 — the placebo, at the mask rather than at the statistic.** Fed the clean
  stack, the mask removes **< 1%** of total admitted weight. If it removes more,
  the normalisation is wrong rather than the stack (the brief's instruction).
- **B3 — the mask must act where the problem is.** On the motion stacks it must
  remove **≥ 5×** the weight fraction it removes on `109MSDCF`. A mask that
  removes the same everywhere is a contrast detector, not a motion detector.
- **B4 — planted sensitivity, with M28's plant and the `s = 0` placebo.**
  Detection must be monotone in shift `s`, and `s = 0` must be
  indistinguishable from no plant at all.

### 0.6 A1c — added after A1 failed, criterion fixed before the run that decides it

A1 failed high, and not marginally: mean chi2 = 1.8508 at gate 1.0 on `109MSDCF`,
the corpus's *quietest* stack, against a pre-registered band of [0.80, 1.30].
Something is inflating the statistic on a stack that is supposed to be the
placebo. Under the brief's own instruction -- "if it does not, the normalisation
is wrong rather than the stack" -- I am obliged to find out which before I read
anything else.

A1b (the photometric rival) was already pre-registered in 0.3 and is now wired
in as a paired arm. But there is a second rival that 0.3 did not anticipate, and
it is the more likely one, so I am registering it here **as a post-hoc
hypothesis with a criterion fixed before I look at its numbers**. Labelling it
honestly matters more than pretending I foresaw it.

**The hypothesis.** The gate admits a frame when its *regularised* focus
response cannot be separated from the winner's by the noise model. The
regulariser is a 17x17 box at radius 8. Two frames whose 17x17-averaged focus
responses agree to within the tolerance can still hold visibly different pixel
values at a sharp edge -- because agreeing about *how much detail is in this
neighbourhood* is not the same claim as agreeing about *what this pixel is*. If
that is what chi2 is reading, then the excess is residual defocus difference
among admitted frames: it is the very signal the merge exists to average down,
not an outlier, and masking it would be masking the estimator's own working set.

**The discriminator.** Residual defocus needs detail to exist. It must vanish
where there is nothing to be defocused. Motion and photometric drift need not.
So bucket the pixels by the winner's own regularised focus response and read
chi2 per bucket. The lowest bucket -- winner focus response below 0.01, i.e. at
or under `FOCUS_NOISE_SD` = 0.0152 -- is flat field, where the null genuinely
applies.

**Criterion A1c, fixed now:**

- **A1c-i (the real placebo).** On `109MSDCF`, mean chi2 in the lowest detail
  bucket lands in **[0.80, 1.30]**. If it does, the noise normalisation is
  correct where the null applies, and A1's failure is a property of the pixels
  and not of my arithmetic. If it does *not*, the normalisation is wrong, I stop
  and fix that, and every number above is void.
- **A1c-ii (the attribution).** Mean chi2 rises **monotonically** across the six
  detail buckets, and the top populated bucket exceeds the bottom by at least
  **3x**. If it does, the excess is attributable to detail, i.e. to residual
  defocus, and a robustness mask keyed on raw chi2 would be keyed on sharpness.
- If A1c-i passes and A1c-ii fails (chi2 flat in detail), the excess is *not*
  detail-driven and the motion/photometric reading survives.

A1b keeps its 0.3 criterion: if removing a per-frame scalar gain removes >= 50%
of the excess over 1.0, the repair is section 8 item 5's photometric
normalisation and not a mask, and I stop.

**Order of precedence.** A1c-i first -- it decides whether any other number is
admissible. Then A1b. Then A1c-ii. Then A2/A3 on the motion stacks, and those
are to be read against `109MSDCF`'s *per-detail-bucket* baseline, not against
1.0, because A1 has already established that 1.0 is not the operating null.

### 0.7 A2' and A3-exact — registered after seeing the deciles, before the run that decides them

Two things in the stage-1 output force a further pre-registration. Both are
recorded here **before** the run that resolves them.

**The confound.** A1c established that chi2 rises steeply with the winner's own
focus response. The three stacks do not share a focus-response distribution:
measurable pixels span 0.601..16.56 on `109MSDCF`, 0.564..47.49 on
`coffee-bubbles`, and **1.054**..63.66 on `foam`. `foam` has no low-detail
measurable pixels at all. So the headline cross-stack ratio -- `foam` 23.84
against `109MSDCF` 1.85, 12.88x -- is computed over populations that differ in
the one variable chi2 is known to depend on most. That is exactly discipline
131's shape, and the decile tables already hint at the answer: `109MSDCF`'s
**top** decile (focus >= 1.55) reads 5.989, and `foam`'s **bottom** decile
(focus 1.05..2.53) reads 6.237. Those are the same number to within 4%.

If the 12.88x survives matching on detail, the corpus has a real motion signal
and the mask has a target. If it does not, then "`foam` disagrees 12.9x more
than `109MSDCF`" is a statement about where the two stacks put their edges, and
a mask keyed on chi2 would be a sharpness detector wearing a robustness label.

**Criterion A2' (matched-detail), fixed now.** Bin every measurable pixel by
winner focus response into fixed absolute octaves `[0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32,
64)`, common to all stacks so the axis means one thing. In every octave
populated by both `foam` and `109MSDCF` with at least 10 000 pixels each,
compute the ratio of mean chi2.

- **A2' is met** if the population-weighted mean of those per-octave ratios is
  **>= 3.0**. A motion signal that is real and not a detail artefact should
  survive matching, and 3.0 is a deliberately weak bar against a raw ratio of
  12.88.
- **A2' fails** if that matched ratio is **< 1.5**, which would mean essentially
  all of the cross-stack difference is detail composition. In that case the
  headline is that this corpus's apparent disagreement signal is confounded, the
  mask has no demonstrated target, and I report that and stop.
- Between 1.5 and 3.0 is an equivocal band and I will report it as equivocal
  rather than round it to whichever side suits.

**Criterion A3-exact.** 0.3 defined `reach` as the fraction of **all** pixels
that are both `n_eff >= 2` and above `109MSDCF`'s p99 chi2, and set the stop
threshold at 1.0%. That p99 is now measured: **13.811** at gate 1.0. Fixing it
as the absolute threshold for all three stacks, `reach` is to be counted
exactly, not interpolated from quantiles. `109MSDCF`'s own reach is 1% of its
exposure by construction -- 0.58% -- and that is the floor any other stack must
be read against, not zero.

## 2. Findings

**F1 (MEASURED).** Mean noise-normalised disagreement among the frames the merge
*actually averages* is **1.8508** on `109MSDCF`, **2.1712** on `coffee-bubbles`,
**23.8430** on `foam`. Criterion A1 pre-registered [0.80, 1.30] for the clean
control and **A1 failed high on the placebo stack**, which is what forced
everything in sections 3 and 4.

**F2 (MEASURED).** The excess is **not** photometric. Removing a per-frame
scalar gain, estimated as fact 225 prescribes (the mean over all pixels, never a
subject-centred median), removes **7.1%** of `109MSDCF`'s excess over 1.0.
Criterion A1b required >= 50%. **A1b fails**, and the mechanism M31-V used to
refute fact 220 does not explain this residual.

**F3 (MEASURED, and it contradicts fact 225's implied prescription).** On the
two stacks with real photometric drift the same correction makes disagreement
**worse**: `coffee-bubbles` 2.1712 -> 2.4345 (**-22.5%**, ptp 8.909%), `foam`
23.8430 -> 24.3811 (**-2.4%**, ptp 10.604%). Fact 225 established that a *median
over a subject-centred region* absorbs focus signal into what it calls gain. My
measurement says **the mean over all pixels does too**, on a stack whose focus
sweep moves the global mean by ~9-11%: on a focus stack the per-frame mean
brightness changes *because the focus changed*, so "gain" fitted that way is
partly focus. Fact 225's negative half stands; its positive half needs scoping.
The estimator that would not have this defect has to be fitted on pixels the
sweep does not move, and nobody has one.

**F4 (MEASURED) -- the placebo the brief asked for, and it passes.** Bucketing
by the winner's own regularised focus response, `109MSDCF`'s lowest decile reads
mean chi2 **1.173**, inside A1c-i's [0.80, 1.30]. **The noise normalisation is
correct where the null actually applies.** So A1's failure is a property of the
pixels and not of the arithmetic, and every number here is admissible. This is
the brief's "if it does not, the normalisation is wrong rather than the stack",
answered: the normalisation is right.

**F5 (MEASURED).** chi2 is dominated by **detail**, not by frames. Across
`109MSDCF`'s deciles: 1.173, 1.379, 1.411, 1.413, 1.406, 1.390, 1.379, 1.353,
1.616, **5.989**. Top over bottom is **5.11x**, so A1c-ii's >= 3x is met;
strict monotonicity is not, and I am not rounding that away -- deciles 2-8 span
focus response 0.73..0.98 out of a full range of 0.60..16.56, so they are not a
detail axis at all, they are a near-constant, and the 4% wobble across them is
noise. **A1c-ii is met on magnitude and failed on monotonicity as written.**

**F6 (MEASURED) -- the finding that decided the milestone.** The three stacks do
not share a focus-response distribution. Measurable pixels span 0.601..16.56 on
`109MSDCF`, 0.564..47.49 on `coffee-bubbles` and **1.054**..63.66 on `foam`:
`foam` has essentially no low-detail measurable pixels. Binned into fixed
absolute octaves common to all three (criterion A2'), `foam` against `109MSDCF`
reads:

| octave | `109MSDCF` | `foam` | ratio | n(109) | n(foam) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [1, 2)  |  1.747 |   6.878 | 3.937 | 550 260 |  21 149 |
| [2, 4)  |  4.065 |   7.459 | 1.835 | 184 340 | 234 252 |
| [4, 8)  | 10.045 |  17.589 | 1.751 | 164 653 | 289 642 |
| [8, 16) | 11.646 |  46.008 | 3.951 |  20 011 | 104 060 |

Population-weighted mean ratio: **2.022** weighting by min(n), **2.205** by
`foam`'s populations, **3.124** by `109MSDCF`'s, **2.868** unweighted. The raw
unmatched ratio is **12.883**. So **matching on detail removes 76-84% of the
apparent cross-stack disagreement signal.** A2' pre-registered >= 3.0 as met,
< 1.5 as failed, and [1.5, 3.0] as equivocal with a commitment not to round.
**A2' is equivocal**: three of four weightings land at 2.02-2.87 and the fourth
at 3.12. The weighting that clears the bar is the one dominated by the [1, 2)
octave, where `foam` contributes only 21 149 pixels.

**F7 (MEASURED) -- `coffee-bubbles` is a measured near-no-op.** Raw, it is
**1.173x** the clean control, against criterion A2's requirement of >= 1.5x for
a motion stack: **A2 fails on `coffee-bubbles`.** Matched on detail by the same
octave method it is **1.184x**, and in the [4, 8) octave it is **0.671x** --
*below* the clean control. A stack the brief names as a known motion failure
case is, on this statistic, indistinguishable from the placebo.

**F8 (MEASURED) -- A3-exact.** Fraction of **all** pixels that are both
measurable and above `109MSDCF`'s p99 (13.811, fixed before this arm was run):
`109MSDCF` **0.5834%**, `coffee-bubbles` **0.5986%**, `foam` **1.5023%**. A3
pre-registered a stop at < 1.0% everywhere; `foam` exceeds it, so **A3 does not
trigger the automatic stop**. But the floor is not zero, it is the clean
control's own 0.5834%, and against that floor `coffee-bubbles` has an excess
reach of **+0.015 percentage points** and `foam` of **+0.92**.

**F9 (MEASURED) -- the surface is small exactly where the motion is.** Fraction
of pixels with `n_eff >= 2`, the entire surface any mask could ever act on:
`109MSDCF` **58.34%**, `coffee-bubbles` **38.26%**, `foam` **8.20%**. On the one
stack in this corpus with undisputed scene motion, **91.80% of pixels averaged
exactly one frame and there is nothing for a robustness mask to reject.** This
is fact 688's shape again and it is why `disagreement` returns `Option` and why
`disagreement_exposure` is published beside every aggregate here.

**F10 (DERIVED, and it is the strongest argument against building this).** On
`foam` at gate 1.0 the measurable pixels are 7.8% at `n_eff = 2` and 0.4% at
`n_eff = 3`; `n_eff >= 4` rounds to 0.0%. So the mask's whole achievable action
is at `n = 2`, where -- with the winner structurally protected by criterion B1 --
there is exactly one move: drop the other frame. For a winner `w` and other `o`
separated by a systematic `d` with per-sample noise `sigma`:

- keep both: `MSE = (d/2)^2 + sigma^2/2`
- winner only: `MSE = sigma^2`
- and `E[chi2] = d^2/(2 sigma^2) + 1`

so dropping lowers MSE **exactly when `E[chi2] > 2`**, with no free parameter.
That threshold is not a tuning knob, it is forced. And it is catastrophic here:
at `n = 2` the realised statistic is one degree of freedom, so under the pure
noise null `P(chi2_1 > 2) = 0.1573`. **A per-pixel binary robustness decision at
its own MSE-optimal threshold has a 15.7% false-positive rate by construction,
on a perfectly clean stack, before any motion exists.** Measured against that
threshold, ~24% of `109MSDCF`'s measurable pixels already exceed 2.0 (p75 =
1.921). Reverting a quarter of the clean control's averaged pixels to single
frames is the failure the governing invariant exists to forbid, and `strehl`'s
entire thesis is that selection is the thing to avoid.

**F11 (ARGUED, from F10).** This is why Wronski et al. pool **per tile** and not
per pixel: spatial pooling buys the degrees of freedom that make the decision
resolvable. But their setting has every frame present in every tile, and here
`n_eff` varies pixel to pixel from 1 to 20 and is 1 over most of the frame. A
tile in this merge is not a sample of `N` frames, it is a ragged mixture, and
pooling it pools pixels whose admitted sets are different. **The reference
design's key enabling assumption does not hold at this seam.** That is a
statement about where the mask belongs -- before or with alignment, on the raw
stack, where every frame is present at every pixel -- not that it is worthless.

becomes 2.0-3.1 under every weighting, and that is the finding.

against the backup, then a full re-run at 37/37.

**Two fixtures failed on their own rule-36 guards before any of this**, which is
the best evidence I have that the guards are not decoration:

- `tracking_disagreement_cannot_change_the_merged_image` panicked with **"no
  pixel admitted a second frame"**. Its focus spacing put every frame outside
  the gate, so it would have compared two identical single-frame merges and
  passed forever while testing nothing.
- `disagreement_fixture` panicked on `n_eff`. My `disagreement_is_none...` test
  assumed a zero-tolerance gate admits only the winner; with a *constant* focus
  field every frame ties the winner exactly and `|a - b| <= 0` admits all of
  them. The test would have been `Some(...)` where it asserted `None`.

**One plant defect found and kept rather than tuned away.** The exact-arithmetic
tests read 49.739 where the hand-computed sample variance is 50. That 0.52%
shortfall is `unit_sigma_model`'s shot term at luma ~105 -- the helper's comment
claims it is "effectively flat in signal", and at these levels it is flat to
0.5%, not to f64. Rather than move the fixture's signal down until the number
looked clean, the assertion now pins the shortfall **directionally**: shot noise
can only raise sigma and therefore only lower chi2, so the test asserts `a <
50.0 && (50.0 - a)/50.0 < 6e-3`. A bracket alone would have passed with the sign
inverted.

## 5. What I did not build, and why

The brief authorised a stop: *"if real stacks show almost no noise-normalised
disagreement, then the mask is machinery for a problem this corpus does not
have"*. That is **not** what I found -- there is plenty of disagreement. I am
stopping for a different and stronger reason, and it is worth stating precisely
because it is not the reason that was offered to me:

1. **The statistic is dominated by detail, not by frames** (F5, F6). 76-84% of
   the apparent cross-stack signal is focus-response composition. A mask keyed on
   raw chi2 is substantially a sharpness detector wearing a robustness label.
2. **One of the two named motion stacks is a measured no-op** (F7). 1.18x
   matched, and below the control in one octave.
3. **The surface is 8.20% exactly where the motion is worst** (F9).
4. **At `n = 2`, which is essentially all of `foam`'s surface, the MSE-optimal
   threshold is forced to `E[chi2] > 2` and carries a 15.7% false-positive rate
   under the pure-noise null** (F10). The brief warned the mask could violate
   the governing invariant "by rejecting good frames in exactly the low-texture
   regions where disagreement is hardest to distinguish from noise". The
   measurement says the risk is real but the region is **high**-texture, not low:
   chi2 rises 5.11x with detail, so the pixels a raw-chi2 mask rejects hardest
   are the sharp ones the whole pipeline exists to preserve.

I want to be exact about the scope of this. **This is not a finding that
robustness masking is worthless for this corpus.** It is a finding that *at this
seam* -- after the gate, on the admitted set, per pixel -- the statistic cannot
carry the decision. F11 argues the seam is wrong rather than the idea: on the
raw stack every frame is present at every pixel, `n` is the frame count and not a
ragged 1-to-20 mixture, and the degrees of freedom that F10 shows are missing are
available. That is where `foam`'s motion should be caught, and it is a
pre-alignment problem, not a merge-time one.

## 6. What I built instead, and the seam decision

### 6.1 The architectural seam, decided explicitly

The brief asked me to decide, and argue, whether the merge carries per-pixel
sufficient statistics or goes two-pass, because section 8 item 6's through-focus
neighbourhood needs the same thing. **Decision: carry sufficient statistics, do
not add a third pass.** The argument:

- **Detection is genuinely free of passes.** `sum` and `sumsq` of the admitted
  green luma are a sufficient statistic for the variance. They are
  order-independent, they absorb additively across workers, and they need no
  frame ever to be revisited. This is asserted, not assumed:
  `disagreement_absorbs_additively_and_matches_a_sequential_fold` builds one
  estimator over frames `[0..6]` and another as `[4,1,5]` absorbing `[3,0,2]`,
  and requires every measurable pixel to agree to 1e-9.
- **Correction is not free, and that asymmetry is the whole result.** A variance
  says *that* the admitted set disagrees. It cannot say *which* frame to
  down-weight, because the per-frame residuals were summed away. Recovering them
  needs a third pass, or per-frame state (the `4.N` bytes/px focus volume this
  project bans), or a winner-sample reference captured in pass one -- which is a
  `merge_cmd.rs` change I do not own (section 7).
- **The codebase has already made this choice once and it worked.** `--stats` /
  `ThroughFocus` carries 60 B/px of per-pixel sufficient statistics at a 5-pixel
  focus window and gets parabola fit, top-4 and bimodality out of it with no
  third pass (fact 421: *"the seam is two-pass, and the per-pixel state is 60
  bytes, constant in the frame count"*). Section 8 item 6 is the same shape and
  should be answered the same way. Fact 152's caveat carries over unchanged:
  bimodality needs statistics at a materially finer scale than the focus window.
- **The honest cost.** `track_disagreement` adds 16 B/px, which at 2344 x 3516
  is **131.9 MB**, taking one estimator from 206.1 MB to **337.9 MB** -- a
  **64% increase**, measured via `allocated_bytes`. Free in passes is not free
  in memory, and the `--stats` help text already records a prior defect where a
  footprint was under-reported by 6.7%, so `allocated_bytes` was updated to
  include the new accumulators rather than left to drift.

Because stage 1 did not authorise the mask, this seam resolved cheaply: the
instrument needed for the *measurement* is exactly the sufficient-statistic
answer, and no third pass was ever required.

### 6.2 `Estimator::track_disagreement` and friends (`merge.rs`)

Opt-in, off by default, and it **cannot change the merged image** -- asserted
sample-by-sample across all four planes in
`tracking_disagreement_cannot_change_the_merged_image`.

- `track_disagreement()` / `tracks_disagreement()` -- allocate, panic if called
  after the first fold.
- `disagreement(&noise, i, black) -> Option<f64>` -- `s^2 / sigma_pred^2`,
  `None` where `n_eff < 2`.
- `disagreement_exposure() -> f64` -- the fraction of pixels where the above is
  defined, i.e. the entire surface any mask could act on. F9 is why this is a
  method and not a comment.
- `fold_censored_probing_photometry(...)` -- applies a per-frame scale to the
  disagreement accumulator **only**, so a photometric hypothesis can be priced
  without proposing a change to the image. F2/F3 are what it is for.

Two numerics notes, both load-bearing:

- The accumulators are `f64` and the existing `f32` `sum` is deliberately **not**
  reused for the mean. Over 100 frames `sumsq` reaches ~2.4e10 against a
  recovered variance of ~1e3: the subtraction cancels about seven significant
  figures, which is all an `f32` has. Reusing the `f32` `sum` would inject ~1.7%
  error into a ~1600 DN^2 variance.
- The luma is captured from the two green samples **inside** the existing
  per-slot loop, at the moment they are added to `sum`, so the variance cannot
  drift from the mean it belongs to.

### 6.3 `crates/strehl-cli/examples/m60-disagreement.rs`

The instrument, mirroring `merge_cmd.rs::field_for` exactly and deliberately
serial. Reports exposure, mean, quantiles, per-`n_eff` and per-detail buckets,
fixed octaves and A3 reach. **Reads only; it never writes to the stack
directory.** Note it required creating `crates/strehl-cli/examples/`, which did
not exist; it touches none of M53's named files and needs no `Cargo.toml` entry,
but it is a new directory in a crate I do not own and I flag it rather than
assume it was mine to make.

### 6.4 Plant disclosure (rule 34 / discipline 358)

`chi2` divides by `sigma_pred` from the calibrated noise model, so **no plant
here is generated by scaling `sigma_pred`** -- that is fact 738's withdrawal.
Concretely, which constants each fixture shares with the code under test:

- The **corpus measurements** move no synthetic content at all. Every number in
  section 2 is real frames through the real pipeline; the only injected quantity
  is the per-frame scalar of F2/F3, which is fitted from the frames themselves.
- `unit_sigma_model` **does** share the noise model with the code, deliberately:
  it sets `sigma_L = 1.0` so `chi2` is the sample variance and the expected
  values are hand-computable integers. Its power is over the *variance
  arithmetic* and it has **none** over the normalisation, which is why NC1 is
  aimed at a different test.
- `shot_noise_model` uses `gain_dn_per_e = 0.3273` and `read_noise_e = 1.24`.
  These are the A7C II's order of magnitude but are **not read from
  `profile.rs`**, precisely so that a wrong constant there cannot make these
  tests pass. They are not a claim about the sensor.
- The two tests with power over the normalisation assert a **ratio between two
  pixels**, so `sigma_pred` either cancels exactly (same mean luma, ratio 4.0) or
  is the only thing that can produce the effect (same variance, decade apart in
  signal). Neither shares a constant with what it is testing.

**I did not depend on `FOCUS_NOISE_SD`.** It sets the gate tolerance and
therefore which frames are admitted, so it moves the *population* every number
here is computed over; it does not enter `chi2`. If M48's re-derivation changes
it, section 2 must be re-run, but nothing here is arithmetically coupled to it.

## 8. Contradictions with the corpus

**C1.** **Fact 225 needs scoping.** It prescribes fitting per-frame gain as the
mean over all pixels, on the evidence that a subject-centred median absorbs focus
signal. F3 measures the prescribed estimator making disagreement **worse** on
both stacks that have real photometric drift (-22.5% and -2.4%). The negative
half of fact 225 stands; the positive half does not generalise to a focus stack,
where the global mean moves because the focus moved.

**C2.** **`coffee-bubbles` should not be cited as a motion positive control**
without qualification (F7). At matched detail it is 1.18x the clean stack and
below it in one octave. Whatever bubble motion is present is not visible to a
noise-normalised disagreement statistic at merge time -- most likely because the
gate already declined to admit the frames that show it, which would make the
gate an outlier rejector nobody has credited as one.

**C3.** **`foam`'s 12.88x should not be quoted unqualified** (F6). It is 2.0-3.1x
at matched detail. This is the same defect shape as my own M58 fact 768 finding:
one number, two meanings, and the difference is a factor of four to six.

**C4 (not a contradiction, a gap).** M28's rows are all marked NOT INDEPENDENTLY
VERIFIED and its section 8 says *"No merge output was produced. This measures a
detector, not the effect of masking on a merged image."* That remains true of
**this** milestone too, and I want it on the record rather than implied: I have
measured a detector and derived its operating point (F10). **I have not produced
a merged image with and without a mask.** Question 45 -- *"does masking actually
improve the merge"* -- is narrowed by F10's forced threshold and its 15.7% null
false-positive rate, but it is not closed by measurement, and the honest reason
is that there is no ground truth in this corpus to score a merged output against.
