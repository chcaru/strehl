# M113-ca: independent falsification of the lateral-CA gain (facts 1511-1514)

**Verdict: DEFECTS FOUND. The gain of 1.444 is not reproducible, the recommended
fix must not ship, and the qualitative conclusion survives.**

ORCH claimed the shipped lateral-CA correction has the correct radial shape but a
scalar gain of 1.444, over-correcting by ~44 %, implying a red-coefficient fix of
x0.692. Measured independently, in a measurement space with no cross-channel
operator in it, on two stacks:

| | needed ppm | shipped ppm | gain | after ORCH x0.692 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bee-3-cpl | 529.9 | 704.5 | **1.2496** | 0.8647 |
| couch-fibers-cpl | 654.9 | 704.5 | **1.0557** | **0.7305** |

The gain does not transfer. ORCH's own pre-registered condition was "if the gain
is not ~1.44 on a second subject, my recommended fix must not ship". It is 1.056.

---

## 1. The measurement space was wrong, and it is the whole error

Facts 1511-1514 were measured on M114's `off.tif`/`on.tif`, rendered with
`--srgb --auto-exposure`. Two cross-channel operators sit between the sensor and
those files, and a lateral-CA measurement is a cross-channel measurement.

**The colour matrix.** `--srgb` applies `color::camera_to_srgb`. Derived from
`color.rs`'s own `A7C2_XYZ_TO_CAM` (my derivation reproduces the crate's asserted
neutral-response constant 1.07304438742 against the test's 1.0730):

```
R_out = +1.636062*R_cam  -0.448619*G_cam  -0.187443*B_cam
G_out = -0.115165*R_cam  +1.488971*G_cam  -0.373806*B_cam
B_out = -0.004740*R_cam  -0.323601*G_cam  +1.328341*B_cam
```

Output red carries camera green at 27.4 % of its own weight; output green carries
camera red at 7.7 % of its own weight. Both at zero displacement. The CLI's own
help for `--srgb` says "For looking at, NOT for measuring."

**CORRECTED 2026-08-24, and the error was mine.** This section first carried
`R_out = +1.1975 R -0.1250 G -0.0725 B` with leaks of 10.4 % and 20.3 %. That is
`rownorm(inv(A@S))`. `color.rs:191-208` computes `inv(rownorm(A@S))` --
row-normalise, *then* invert -- and the two do not commute. The independent
witness that settles it is the crate's own doc comment at `color.rs:221`, which
states the green row as `[-0.115, +1.489, -0.374]`: the corrected form reproduces
it to 1.94e-4, the old form misses it by 0.741. Recomputed from the crate's own
constants by `experiments/M113-ca/src/c12_matrixcheck.py`.

**No measured number in this report changes.** The matrix appears here only as
explanation; it is never applied in any script, and every figure was measured on
files the crate itself rendered using its own correct matrix. The conclusion is
strengthened rather than weakened: the green-to-red leak that makes `--srgb`
unfit for a cross-channel measurement is **27.4 %**, not 10.4 %.

**The demosaic.** Both `--srgb` and `--demosaic` go through Malvar-He-Cutler.
`demosaic.rs` documents its own assumption in its own test name:

> "Malvar-He-Cutler's gradient correction assumes the *colour difference* R - G
> is smoother than either channel, which is a property of real scenes and not a
> theorem."

A lateral-CA fringe *is* roughness in `R - G` at an edge. The demosaic's core
assumption is violated by exactly the signal being measured, and it smooths that
signal toward zero.

**The calibration that settles it.** `applied = eps_on - eps_off` has a known
truth: the ON arm warps red by the compile-time constant `eps_r = -704.5 ppm`.
So `applied / eps_r` measures the transfer of the whole measurement chain, and a
space that returns 1.00 is uncontaminated. Same estimator, same stack, three
spaces:

| space | cross-channel operators | applied/eps_r |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--srgb --auto-exposure` (what facts 1511-1514 used) | matrix + demosaic | **1.3073** |
| `--demosaic`, linear | demosaic | **0.6963** |
| binned, linear (byte-frozen measurement render) | **none** | **0.9399** |
| binned, linear, couch-fibers-cpl | **none** | **0.9813** |

The two contaminated spaces are wrong in opposite directions, by +31 % and
-30 %. The binned render is the file the CLI help calls "the file every number in
this project's corpus was measured on. It is byte-frozen." All numbers below are
measured there.

## 2. Two corrections against my own earlier report

Both of my previous headline figures were themselves measured in the sRGB space
and both move:

- I reported "the coefficient magnitude is essentially correct, needed 718.7 vs
  shipped 704.5 ppm, 2.0 %". That 2.0 % agreement was a coincidence of the
  colour matrix's +31 % inflation. In the clean space `needed` is 529.9 ppm
  (bee), a 25 % gap, and 654.9 ppm (couch), a 5.6 % gap.
- I reported gain 1.285 and a fix of x0.780. In the clean space it is 1.2496 on
  bee and 1.0557 on couch, and **no scalar fix is supported at all**.
- I reported that ORCH's estimator "is blind to 94 % of the field". That is
  space-specific, not estimator-specific. On the binned render ORCH's own
  unmodified `shift_of` recovers -593.2 ppm against my -654.9 on couch (9.4 %
  apart) and returns gain 1.0595 against my 1.0557. **The estimator is not
  broken; it is unusually sensitive to a contamination that was really there.**
  Its whitening step, `X/|X|`, weights all frequencies equally and therefore
  maximally amplifies the zero-lag component the matrix and the demosaic inject.

## 3. What survives, and it is the part that matters

**Fact 1514 is confirmed and strengthened.** The correction over-corrects and
flips the sign of the residual on both stacks, in the clean space, under both
estimators:

| stack | eps_off ppm | eps_on ppm | residual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bee-3-cpl | -529.9 | **+132.3** | sign flipped, 25 % of original |
| couch-fibers-cpl | -654.9 | **+36.5** | sign flipped, 5.6 % of original |

A sign-flipped residual is precisely M107's `RECOLOURED, NOT REMOVED` and its
75.1 % keep-sign failure. ORCH's mechanism is right.

**The radial shape is right.** `eps(r)`, formed per annulus as
`median[(d - t).rhat / |p - c|]` with the global translation removed, is flat
across the entire field on the better-conditioned stack:

```
couch-fibers-cpl   r=0.11  0.24  0.38  0.52  0.66  0.77  0.87
eps_off ppm         -677  -660  -647  -649  -655  -662  -670
bootstrap sd          48    12     5     6     9     8    10   <- CORRECTED (block)
[withdrawn]           80    23    12     9     8     8    12   <- as first published
```

**CORRECTED 2026-08-24 -- the sd row above was wrong, and it was too LARGE.**
`c10_rings.py:58-61` drew `rng.integers` **twice**, resampling numerator and
denominator with different indices, so every bootstrap replicate divided window
*i*'s radial displacement by window *j*'s radius. `c13_bootaudit.py` reproduces
the published row exactly (80.0 at r=0.11) and then recomputes it correctly.
`c14_pairmech.py` shows the inflation is driven by each ring's relative radius
spread -- Spearman rho **+0.93** (bee-3) and **+0.82** (couch-fibers) over 7
rings -- which is why the innermost ring, spanning a max/min of 6.46, was hit
hardest and the rim was barely touched.

The corrected row is the **block** bootstrap, which resamples contiguous spatial
blocks and so does not assume neighbouring windows are independent. It is larger
than the textbook paired bootstrap (33.5 against 48.2 at r=0.11, 1.44x): window
correlation is real and does inflate the honest bar, just not to 80.

**The point estimates are untouched** -- `c10:55` was correctly paired; only the
`+/-` column was affected. **The conclusion survives** and was re-tested rather
than assumed: `c15_flatness.py` fits a constant `eps` by inverse-variance
weighting and gets **chi2/dof = 1.19** on couch-fibers against the corrected
bars (0.57 against the inflated ones), peak-to-peak 4.7 % of the scale. On
bee-3 the naive paired bootstrap would have **rejected** flatness at 3.17; the
block bootstrap restores it at 1.56. Smaller bars make a flatness claim harder,
so this had to be checked in the direction that could have overturned it.

**B7, centre behaviour.** My pre-registered bar said "a real scale must produce a
vanishing radial displacement at the centre". That bar was mis-stated: the
innermost ring is not the centre, it sits at r ~ 230 px where a -516 ppm scale
predicts -0.12 px. Against the correct prediction:

| stack | predicted px | measured px | error px | tangential floor px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bee-3-cpl | -0.1220 | -0.1065 | 0.0155 | 0.0128 |
| couch-fibers-cpl | -0.1523 | -0.1528 | **0.0005** | 0.0170 |

The restatement is forced by arithmetic, not chosen to pass, so both forms are
recorded.

## 5. Why a scalar cannot be fitted on one scene

The two stacks need coefficients 529.9 and 654.9 ppm, a spread of 23.6 %, with
per-ring bootstrap sds of 8-23 ppm - the difference is ~140 ppm and is far
outside measurement noise. Lateral CA is the separation of the *effective
wavelength* of the red channel from that of the green, and those effective
wavelengths are each channel's passband centroid **weighted by the scene's own
spectrum**. The two scenes are differently coloured:

| stack | R/G | B/G |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bee-3-cpl | 1.0017 | 0.9334 |
| couch-fibers-cpl | 0.9381 | 1.0807 |

so they genuinely need different coefficients through the same glass. This is
fact 52's warning made concrete and measured.

Residual multiplier after each candidate fix - 1.00 is perfect, above 1
over-corrects, below 1 under-corrects:

| stack | keep x1.000 | ORCH x0.692 | my earlier x0.780 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bee-3-cpl | 1.2496 | 0.8647 | 0.9747 |
| couch-fibers-cpl | 1.0557 | **0.7305** | 0.8234 |

ORCH's x0.692 turns couch-fibers-cpl's 5.6 % over-correction into a **27.0 %
under-correction** - larger in magnitude than the error it was meant to remove.

## 6. Defect in ca_gain.py

`ca_gain.py` computes

```python
resid_pred = abs(w["R_off"] * (1.0 - np.median(g)/np.median(g)))
```

which is identically zero by construction, and prints it as
`median |residual| if gain were 1.0 : %.3f px (i.e. at the estimator's floor)`.
It reports 0.000 on any input. It is not evidence of anything.

## 7. What remains unverified

- Whether the align stage can introduce inter-channel radial displacement. I
  argue it cannot, because a per-frame similarity moves all photosites together,
  but I did not test it. If it can, part of `needed` is not optical.
- Two stacks is two. The 23.6 % spread is established; its cause is attributed to
  scene spectrum by argument and by the channel-ratio table, not by a
  spectrally-controlled experiment.
- The binned space's chain transfer is 0.94 and 0.98, not 1.00. The residual
  2-6 % is unexplained and bounds the absolute accuracy of `needed`. It does not
  affect `gain`, which is a ratio in which the transfer cancels.

## 10. Disciplines

11. **Two independent `rng.integers` draws inside one bootstrap replicate is a
    silent pairing defect.** It does not error, it does not look wrong, and it
    produces a plausible, conservative-looking bar. Draw the index set **once**
    and index every array with it.
12. **An error bar that is too LARGE is not the safe direction.** It buys an
    unearned pass for any flatness, null or agreement claim tested against it.
    When a bar is corrected downward, every conclusion that rested on it has to
    be re-tested in the direction that could overturn it.
13. **A commissioning control must exercise the mechanism, not merely the
    form.** C13's control used a radius spread of 1.67x where the real innermost
    ring spans 6.46x, so it measured 2.03x against a 3.0x bar and FAILED --
    while the real defect was larger than the control could produce. Chasing the
    failure to relative radius spread is what commissioned the audit.
14. **When two suspected errors act on the same number, do not reason about the
    net.** The pairing defect inflated by 2.4x and the correlation the naive
    bootstrap ignored deflated by 1.44x. Neither the sign nor the size of the
    combination was predictable from either argument alone.
