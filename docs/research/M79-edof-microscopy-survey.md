**MEASURED (repository audit, 2026-08-23).** The premise in the commission is

## 0. Verdict first

**ARGUED (M79).** The microscopy literature does **not** overturn M70. It makes
M70's `PLUGIN` verdict stronger, while sharpening where that verdict does and
does not apply.

**ARGUED (M79).** Microscopy supports deconvolution as the core reconstruction
when the wanted object is a **3-D additive fluorophore volume**, the PSF is
known or tightly constrained, z is calibrated, and the optics and sample make a
linear convolution model defensible. That is not this rig. For a **2-D
all-in-focus image of an opaque surface**, the closest primary source is Aguet
et al.'s joint texture/topography estimator. Even there, the exact
deconvolution update is deferred to the final fine scale, its large numerical
advantage is measured on data generated by its own forward model, and its real
comparisons are qualitative.

**MEASURED (M70).** M70's own P4 confirms the premise that one- and two-step
off-peak frames retain substantial mid-band amplitude: 0.879–0.970 and
0.461–0.858 respectively. M70's own P3 then measures why that does not imply a
new core: the admitted spectra are nearly scaled copies, with leading
singular-value share 0.9860–0.9983 and median 0.9959. M70's own P1 prices the
best matched filter against the best equal average at 1.110×, or +0.91 dB,
while the shipped merge already supplies +5.45 dB. M70's own P24 gives a
second route: a one-response filter is mis-specified on multi-lobed,
multiple-depth patches. M70's own P31–P32 give a third: on M73's two stacks,
widening the gate closes 74–81% of the gap and the matched filter contributes
only the remaining +0.76 to +0.79 dB, reproducing the corpus headroom at
1.092–1.096×. [M70, §0, §19.8, §21.6–§21.7]

**MEASURED (Sage et al.).** The closest independent numerical echo is from
volumetric fluorescence, not EDOF fusion: on Sage et al.'s synthetic
microtubule volume, a 7-second regularised inverse filter scored 3.47 dB SNR
and 1661-second Richardson–Lucy scored 3.66 dB. RL-TV scored 3.36 dB.
[Sage-2017, Table 2 and §5.1]

**DERIVED (M79 from Sage et al. Table 2).** The nonlinear iteration bought
0.19 dB over the one-shot inverse, and the TV-regularised variant did not beat
plain RL on that filamentary object.

**ARGUED (M79).** That agreement is not proof that every nonlinear inverse
method is worthless. It is evidence that, once the useful frames carry almost
collinear spectra, algorithmic sophistication mainly changes the prior and
regularisation, not the information supplied by the stack. A learned or
object-specific prior can still win; that is a different product claim from
"off-peak frames require deconvolution as the core merge."

## 1. The two problems called EDOF

### 1.1 Volumetric deconvolution

**MEASURED (Sage et al.).** Widefield fluorescence deconvolution models the
observed z-volume as a 3-D fluorophore distribution convolved with a 3-D PSF,
plus noise. Sage et al. formulate `y = Hx + n`; their §3 distinguishes
Gaussian least squares, Tikhonov and derivative regularisation, Landweber,
FISTA, Poisson Richardson–Lucy, and RL-TV. Their §4.2 says deconvolution
quality depends on PSF accuracy and describes either bead-measured or
theoretically generated PSFs. [Sage-2017, §§3.1–3.9, 4.1–4.2]

**ARGUED (M79).** The output is a restored **volume**. A maximum-intensity or
other EDOF projection, if desired, comes afterwards. Calling deconvolution the
"core" is correct for that objective, but it does not answer how an opaque
surface stack should become one 2-D image.

### 1.2 Thin-surface EDOF

**MEASURED (Forster et al.; Aguet et al.).** Brightfield EDOF usually assumes
that the wanted information lies on one surface. Forster et al. select
high-frequency coefficients from the best-looking slice. Aguet et al. instead
represent a texture on a continuous height field, convolve that thin surface
with a 3-D PSF, and jointly estimate texture and topography.
[Forster-2004, Introduction and Methods; Aguet-2008, §§I–III]

**ARGUED (M79).** `strehl` belongs much closer to this second lineage, but not
perfectly. An opaque macro scene is surface-like away from occlusions. At an
occluding edge it is explicitly **not** one surface per sensor pixel, which is
the exact place where the thin-surface approximation fails.

## 3. Structured survey by line of work

### 3.1 Wavelet and complex-wavelet EDOF

**MEASURED (Forster et al.: problem).** Forster et al. address a registered
microscopy z-stack in which different surface regions are sharp in different
slices. Their algorithm converts multichannel data to a scalar channel,
computes a complex wavelet transform per slice, and chooses the coefficient
with maximum magnitude at each subband/location. It then inverts the transform
and can reassign the result to observed multichannel values.
[Forster-2004, “Image Fusion Algorithm,” “Selection Rule and Consistency
Checks,” and “Pre- and Postprocessing Steps”]

**MEASURED (Forster et al.: forward model).** This is not an inverse optical
model. The focus premise is that sharp regions have larger high-frequency
coefficients. The paper requires registered images, constant magnification,
z-steps smaller than the depth of field, and preferably opaque,
nontransparent, nonreflective specimens with one focus position per location.
[Forster-2004, Methods and Discussion]

**DERIVED (M79 from Forster et al. Tables 1–2).** On four simulated
Brodatz-texture stacks, complex wavelets gained 3.21 dB over local variance
without post-processing and 4.70 dB with subband consistency plus
reassignment. On the separate **simulated stack made from histological tissue
texture**, the corresponding gains were 0.64 dB and 3.41 dB. Complex wavelets
without post-processing beat real wavelets by about 0.7–1.2 dB in those tests.
[Forster-2004, Tables 1–2]

**MEASURED (Forster et al.: real-data boundary).** Real-specimen comparisons
were visual, not ground-truth measurements. [Forster-2004, Results]

**MEASURED (Forster et al.: failure).** Maximum-magnitude coefficient selection
puts about 30% of typical fused pixels outside every input image's dynamic
range, increasing energy, noise, and saturation. Reassignment reduces ringing
by snapping to an available source value. The authors also warn that maximum
selection can enhance noise and suggest wavelet denoising.
[Forster-2004, “Postprocessing: Reassignment” and Discussion]

**ARGUED (M79: transfer).** This family is a sophisticated selector, not the
latent estimator sought by `strehl`. It needs no PSF or calibrated z, which is
attractive here, but its acquisition assumptions are violated by focus
breathing and occluding geometry, and its noise remedy is heuristic. Its main
transferable lesson is negative: transform-domain selection can invent
out-of-range energy, so range safety and propagated uncertainty need to be
structural.

### 3.2 Model-based 2.5-D brightfield EDOF

**MEASURED (Aguet et al.: problem and model).** Aguet et al. jointly estimate a
continuous topography and a surface texture. The object is a Dirac surface in
z; image formation is its 3-D convolution with the microscope PSF, followed by
sampling. Alternating least squares updates texture and topography, with
coarse-to-fine B-spline topography. Under additive white Gaussian noise the
authors interpret the optimization as maximum likelihood / EM with topography
as hidden state. [Aguet-2008, §§II–III]

**MEASURED (Aguet et al.: deconvolution placement).** At coarse scales the
simplified texture update is a weighted interpolation among nearby z-slices.
The exact gradient-based PSF update, which supplies deconvolution, is used only
at the final fine scale after topography has converged.
[Aguet-2008, §III.A.2 and §III.D]

**MEASURED (Aguet et al.: synthetic comparison).** On an eight-slice,
256×256 dome generated by the paper's own thin-surface convolution model,
Table I reports texture/topography SNR of 32.07/26.94 dB for local variance,
32.41/35.89 dB for CWT-EDF, and 54.22/53.36 dB for the model-based method.
Section V.A does not report adding a sensor-noise realization, and every
position was deliberately represented in focus. [Aguet-2008, §V.A, Fig. 5,
Table I]

**DERIVED (M79 from Aguet Table I) + ARGUED (transfer).** The 21.81 dB texture
advantage over CWT-EDF is real for the paper's test, but the generator and
estimator share the same one-surface model. It measures exact-model recovery
and continuous-z interpolation, not robustness to an unknown photographic
PSF, focus breathing, occlusion, Bayer sampling, or registration residuals.

**MEASURED (Aguet et al.: real data).** The experimental stacks used a 0.75 NA
air objective with 16 sections at 3 µm and a 0.1 NA air objective with 32
sections at 20 µm. The real comparison to CWT-EDF is qualitative: Figs. 7–8
show fewer colour/misselection artifacts and sharper cell boundaries; no
ground-truth score is reported. The method took 64 seconds versus 16 seconds
for CWT-EDF in the reported timing comparison. [Aguet-2008, §V.B and §VI.B]

**MEASURED (Aguet et al.: model sensitivity).** Simulated topography was
reported insensitive to as much as 30% error in the Gaussian PSF-width
parameter. The authors immediately narrow that result: the in-focus PSF must be
accurate, or deconvolution is too weak or reallocates out-of-focus information
falsely into the texture. Their transmission model explicitly ignores
beneath-surface contributions. [Aguet-2008, §II and §VI.A]

**ARGUED (M79: transfer).** This is genuine prior art for "estimation, not
selection." Its most transferable core is continuous sub-frame topography plus
range-safe interpolation. Its least transferable part is non-blind
deconvolution under a fitted microscope PSF. At this rig, the paper supports a
cheap interpolation stage and an optional inverse refinement after model
validation, not an unconditional inverse as the merge core.

### 3.3 3-D deconvolution: inverse, Wiener, RL, and regularisation

**MEASURED (Sage et al.: methods).** Sage et al. compare naive inverse,
Tikhonov, regularised inverse, Landweber, Tikhonov–Miller, FISTA,
Richardson–Lucy, and RL-TV under one 3-D convolution model. Their regularised
inverse becomes Wiener filtering when the derivative prior whitens the object
and the regularisation is set from inverse noise variance. RL is a Poisson
maximum-likelihood iteration; early stopping or explicit regularisation is
needed to avoid noise amplification. [Sage-2017, §§3.2–3.9]

**MEASURED (Sage et al.: benchmark).** Their synthetic 128×512×256
microtubule volume was degraded with a theoretical PSF and mixed Gaussian plus
Poisson noise. RIF/RL/RL-TV scored 3.47/3.66/3.36 dB SNR in
7/1661/2759 seconds. The challenge winner “Learn 2D, Apply 3D” scored 7.27 dB
in 7200 seconds by learning high-resolution lateral features as an axial prior.
Parameters were selected through visual assessment, and the authors warn that
the ranking depends on object structure. [Sage-2017, §5.1, Figs. 4–5,
Table 2]

**MEASURED (Sage et al.: real calibration).** Their bead and C. elegans
examples use a standard widefield microscope at 1.4 NA, 64.5 nm lateral
sampling, and 160 nm z-steps. On the bead, every reported RIF and Landweber
setting made axial FWHM numerically larger than the 3979 nm acquisition value:
4449–5909 nm. The algorithms still sharpened and separated structures
visually in the biological volume. [Sage-2017, §§5.2–5.3, Table 3, Fig. 7]

**MEASURED (Arigovindan et al.).** ER-Decon uses a quadratic data term,
positivity, and a nonquadratic second-derivative/intensity regulariser targeted
at sparse fluorescence structures. It was validated by correlating
low-dose restorations with high-dose acquisitions via Fourier shell
correlation. The paper reports stronger retention than Huygens and
DeconvolutionLab for a 20× dose pair, four yeast-vacuole dose levels, and a
400× Zip1-filament dose pair. [Arigovindan-2013, “Mathematical Formulation”
and “Experimental Validation,” Figs. 1–5]

**ARGUED (M79: transfer).** These papers strongly support inverse restoration
when the wanted object is an additive 3-D volume. Their best gains often come
from a morphology prior, not from extracting novel per-frame spectrum. The
rig's opaque surface, unknown PSF, uncalibrated z, and Bayer sampling break the
load-bearing assumptions. The numerical proximity of RIF and RL is
independent support for trying a one-shot inverse plugin before any iterative
core.

### 3.4 Blind and myopic deconvolution

**MEASURED (Sage et al.).** Blind deconvolution jointly estimates object and
PSF and is strongly ill-posed, including a scale degeneracy. The reviewed
microscopy methods constrain the PSF through optical parameterisation or a
dictionary and alternate object and PSF updates. Sage et al. also note that a
PSF can vary with position and depth because of refractive-index mismatch.
[Sage-2017, §§6.1–6.2]

**MEASURED (Hom et al.: myopic model).** AIDA is myopic rather than
unconstrained blind deconvolution. It uses an ensemble of measured PSFs to
form an OTF mean and frequency-dependent variance, then lets the PSF move
within that harmonic constraint while jointly estimating the object in a MAP
framework. Its data term is weighted by Gaussian read noise plus
signal-dependent Poisson noise. It assumes shift invariance, flat-fielded
pixels, and either a supplied read-noise value or a dark image.
[Hom-2007, §§2.A–2.D, 3.A, 3.C]

**MEASURED (Hom et al.: results).** On a 17 dB synthetic phantom, classical
deconvolution with the average PSF improved SNR by 1.7 dB; myopic restoration
gave 2.9 dB automatically and 4.2 dB after halving one regularisation
parameter, versus 3.8 dB for classical deconvolution with the true PSF at the
same adjusted regularisation. The recovered PSF also absorbed object-like
line artifacts, and the authors state that complete object/PSF separation is
unlikely without stronger constraints. [Hom-2007, §4, Figs. 5–6, Table 1]

**MEASURED (Hom et al.: multi-frame result).** On five adaptive-optics
observations of Uranus, global multi-PSF deconvolution produced about 2–3×
cloud contrast and reduced one ghost to about half the intensity left by
deconvolving the shift-and-add image. This is a real multi-frame comparison,
but it is astronomy with repeated views and measured guide-object PSFs, not a
focus sweep. [Hom-2007, §5.A, Fig. 10]

**ARGUED (M79: transfer).** Myopic deconvolution is the defensible inverse
family for this rig because "known PSF" is false. It is not free: AIDA's
success depends on reference PSF samples and a constrained PSF distribution,
while this rig currently has neither a point target nor calibrated z. A
myopic **plugin experiment** is justified; a blind core that learns both
scene and PSF from one stack is not.

### 3.5 Through-focus PSF modelling

**MEASURED (Aguet et al.).** Aguet et al. fit a computationally cheap,
z-dependent Gaussian to a scalar optical PSF and normalise every xy-section
for Köhler illumination. Their model can instead accept a theoretical or
measured PSF. [Aguet-2008, §IV]

**MEASURED (Ikoma et al.).** Ikoma et al. derive a generalized-aperture PSF
from Debye theory using NA, immersion index, wavelength, magnification,
objective focal length, and pixel integration. They assume a circular
aperture, a thin sample, and a depth-invariant PSF. A distorted bead in their
real result is attributed partly to mismatch between that simulated PSF and
the actual microscope; they propose measured, depth-varying, or blind PSFs as
remedies. [Ikoma-2018, “Point spread function model,” Discussion, Fig. 1]

**MEASURED (Hom et al.).** AIDA's real 3-D chromosome result retained
hourglass blur that grew with depth; the authors attribute it to
refractive-index aberration and violation of the space-invariant PSF.
[Hom-2007, §6.B, Fig. 14]

**ARGUED (M79: transfer).** A microscope objective's NA, immersion index,
telecentric stage motion, and narrow emission band make analytical
through-focus modelling unusually identifiable. A photographic macro lens
plus teleconverter, moved through internal focus, does not expose equivalent
parameters. The first transferable PSF task is therefore empirical:
measure edge/point responses per CFA plane, aperture, focus region, and field
position, then test whether one constrained family predicts held-out frames.
Until that succeeds, non-blind deconvolution is model theatre.

### 3.6 Depth from focus and depth from defocus

**MEASURED (Forster et al.).** Classical microscopy EDF constructs a depth map
by selecting the slice with maximum local variance or high-frequency energy;
wavelet methods make the same choice per scale/subband. The resulting
topography is discrete in frame index. [Forster-2004, Introduction and
“Classical Approach”]

**MEASURED (Aguet et al.).** Aguet et al. replace the discrete index map with
a continuous B-spline topography and jointly adjust it with texture. That
removes staircase quantisation under their thin-surface model.
[Aguet-2008, §§I.B, III.C, V]

**MEASURED (Bhasin and Chaudhuri).** Bhasin and Chaudhuri model focused
intensity and space-varying blur/depth as separate Markov random fields under
two known camera settings and Gaussian observation noise. They maximize the
joint posterior of depth and focused intensity. At a depth discontinuity they
detect the edge and iteratively replace the circular Gaussian blur with a
truncated kernel implied by finite-aperture visibility.
[Bhasin-2001, §§2–4]

**MEASURED (Bhasin and Chaudhuri).** On their synthetic two-plane random-dot
scene, accounting for partial self-occlusion changed whole-image RMS depth
error from 0.0075 to 0.0070. Within ±4 pixels of the discontinuity, RMS
changed from 0.0142 to 0.0080. No real-data validation is reported.
[Bhasin-2001, §5, Figs. 4–7]

**ARGUED (M79: transfer).** Relative depth-from-focus in frame coordinates
transfers without a rail; metric depth-from-defocus does not. Bhasin's result
is important because it proves that finite-aperture visibility belongs in the
forward model, but it assumes calibrated camera settings and a geometric
kernel. At this rig it is a structural precedent, not a ready estimator.

### 3.7 Posterior over depth rather than argmax

**MEASURED (primary sources).** Three reviewed Bayesian-looking methods stop at
a point estimate:

1. Bhasin and Chaudhuri write the joint posterior for blur/depth and focused
   intensity, then compute its MAP state. [Bhasin-2001, §4.1]
2. Aguet et al. interpret alternating least squares as EM with topography as a
   hidden state, but output one continuous topography. [Aguet-2008, §III]
3. AIDA maximizes a posterior over object and PSF, not a frame-of-best-focus
   distribution. [Hom-2007, §2.B]

**MEASURED (M79 search).** The exact OpenAlex query `"depth from focus"
"posterior distribution"` returned nine works; none of the nine titles or
available records concerned a retained per-pixel focus-stack posterior used
for fusion. Europe PMC returned one event-camera review, also not such a
method. Exact Europe PMC searches for `"frame of best focus" posterior` and
`"depth from focus" Bayesian microscopy` returned zero.

**ARGUED (M79).** A MAP formulation is not prior art for the project's proposed
object. To match, a paper must retain the normalized per-frame distribution
and use that **same distribution** for radiance weights, uncertainty, and
occlusion. No reviewed source does so.

### 3.8 Layered occlusion and unmixing

**MEASURED (Hasinoff and Kutulakos: problem).** Variable-aperture photography
uses three images captured at fixed focus and different apertures. Exposure,
defocus, dynamic range, and noise change together. The authors solve for an
all-in-focus HDR radiance map, a layer assignment, per-layer blur, and
front-to-back ordering. [Hasinoff-2007, §§1–2, 5–7]

**MEASURED (Hasinoff and Kutulakos: forward model).** Each layer has radiance
and an alpha matte. The layer's masked radiance is blurred separately, then
multiplied by cumulative occlusion from defocused foreground mattes and
composited. Hidden radiance is extended behind foreground layers by
inpainting. This is an approximate finite-aperture layered model, not an
additive transparent convolution. [Hasinoff-2007, §4, Eqs. 4–5, Figs. 2–4]

**MEASURED (Hasinoff and Kutulakos: inversion and results).** The inverse
alternates radiance restoration, blur refinement, discrete layer refinement,
and depth-order search. Real data came from a tripod-mounted Canon EOS 1Ds
Mark II, 85 mm F1.2 lens, RAW capture, and an f/8–f/4–f/2 aperture bracket.
The paper shows visually realistic boundary resynthesis and correct layer
ordering in its examples; its synthetic Lena resynthesis is visually close
to ground truth, but it reports no scalar boundary-error comparison.
[Hasinoff-2007, §§6–9, Figs. 6–8]

**MEASURED (Hasinoff and Kutulakos: limitation).** The practical reduced model
uses hard layer segmentation and inpainting for unseen radiance. Residual
error is not always discriminative enough to correct wrong labels. The paper
does not vary focus, does not use a Bayer-domain likelihood, and does not
produce calibrated per-pixel uncertainty. [Hasinoff-2007, §§4, 8]

**ARGUED (M79).** This answers the broad novelty question decisively:
structural layer unmixing at defocused occlusion boundaries is prior art.
It does **not** answer the narrower engineering question of recovering two
layers from an uncalibrated focus sweep. The variables and visibility model
transfer; the fixed-focus aperture bracket, calibrated radiometry, and
Gaussian blur law do not.

**ARGUED (M79).** Bhasin's kernel truncation and Hasinoff's layered compositing
are different remedies. The former changes the forward blur near a detected
depth boundary; the latter explicitly estimates separate layer radiance and
visibility. The project's proposed two-layer local solve belongs to the
second family.

**MEASURED (M63).** Local evidence also forbids using a second focus-curve mode
as the layer oracle. M63 §0.1 measured 78.3% bimodality at labelled occlusion
edges and 78.9% at featureless-card silhouettes; only 7.2% of second peaks
landed near independently estimated background focus, and cross-capture
median Cohen's kappa was +0.109. A single-depth defocusing edge generated the
side lobes: on a known two-depth plant they captured 54–68% of detections
while the true background captured 0–1%, although within roughly 8 pixels the
true background was recovered 25–44% of the time.

**ARGUED (M79).** Therefore the import is not "split whenever the curve is
bimodal." It is "fit an occlusion-aware two-layer forward model in a narrow
edge band, and require held-out-frame evidence that the second layer predicts
data better than the single-edge PSF side-lobe model."

### 3.9 Light-field and wavefront-coded EDOF

**MEASURED (Broxton et al.).** A light-field microscope places a microlens
array in the image path, records spatial and angular light, builds a
position-dependent wave-optics measurement matrix, and reconstructs a 3-D
fluorescence volume with a Poisson Richardson–Lucy likelihood. The model
assumes monochromatic fluorescence, no scattering, and no occluding objects;
emitter intensities add. [Broxton-2013, §§3.1–3.4]

**MEASURED (Cohen et al.).** Cohen et al. add cubic or spiral phase masks at the
objective back focal plane and optionally at each microlens. Their
experimental 20×, 0.5 NA water-immersion system used a 125 µm-pitch
microlens array and a fabricated cubic mask. The mask made resolution more
uniform across depth, but peak resolution fell from more than 500 line
pairs/mm to about 350 line pairs/mm, about 64% of the unmasked peak.
[Cohen-2014, §§1–4.2, Fig. 5]

**MEASURED (Cohen et al.).** The phase mask improved reconstructed bead and
zebrafish detail at the native object plane; it did so by changing the PSF and
the information recorded before software reconstruction. [Cohen-2014, §4.3,
Figs. 6–7]

**ARGUED (M79: transfer).** These are valuable contrasts because they show how
microscopy obtains a genuinely different conditioning of the inverse problem:
it changes the optics. `strehl` cannot import that information after capture.
The only transferable pieces are the explicit forward operator, Poisson
likelihood, and Fisher-information habit of asking whether the acquisition
contains the parameter before optimizing an estimator.

### 3.10 Noise-aware inverse imaging

**MEASURED (Forster et al.; Aguet et al.).** Forster's fusion score is
contrast-driven and only adds denoising after selection. Aguet's objective is
unweighted least squares under white Gaussian noise; it does not use a
photon-transfer calibration or spatial PRNU. [Forster-2004, Discussion;
Aguet-2008, §III]

**MEASURED (Hom et al.).** AIDA uses per-pixel data-fidelity weights formed
from Gaussian read-noise variance plus Poisson variance. It expects flat-field
correction; for extended microscopy objects it needs a dark image because
negative background pixels may be unavailable. It does not model residual
PRNU inside the likelihood. [Hom-2007, §§2.A, 3.A, 3.C]

**MEASURED (Ikoma et al.).** Ikoma et al. use a mixed Poisson–Gaussian camera
model, approximate it with a shifted-Poisson likelihood, and estimate camera
gain and read-noise variance from the focal stack with Foi's mean–variance
method. They first apply a dark correction and pixelwise flat-field
correction; their experiments used a 1000-frame dark mean and flat fields
formed from 100 positions. The reconstruction then assumes uniform gain.
[Ikoma-2018, “Image formation and noise model,” “Pre-processing,” Methods]

**MEASURED (Ikoma et al.).** On simulated fluorescence volumes, the
shifted-Poisson term beat Gaussian and Poisson-only terms at low photon counts,
and the Hessian regulariser gave the best or tied-best SNR across the tested
objects/noise levels. The noise was generated from the same
Poisson–Gaussian family. Real comparisons used oil/water high-NA objectives,
motorised or piezo z, and confocal data as an imperfect baseline.
[Ikoma-2018, Table 1, “Experimental validation,” Methods]

**MEASURED (Broxton et al.; Hasinoff and Kutulakos).** Broxton et al. use a
Poisson sensor likelihood and argue that heterogeneous variance across
fluorescence pixels is critical. Hasinoff and Kutulakos calibrate sensor
response and lens attenuation, propagate amplified noise after linearisation,
choose initial source pixels by predicted SNR, and weight TV regularisation by
effective exposure. [Broxton-2013, §3.3–3.4; Hasinoff-2007, §§5–7]

**ARGUED (M79: transfer).** The broad claim "EDOF never uses a sensor noise
model" is false. The defensible claim is narrower: **no obtained and read
thin-surface EDOF fusion drives Bayer-CFA merge weights and output confidence
from an external photon-transfer/read-noise/PRNU calibration.** Kim et al.
2009 has the verified title “Real-time bayer-domain image restoration for an
extended depth of field (EDoF) camera,” but its full text could not be obtained
and no substantive inference is made from its title. AIDA and Ikoma make the
project's claim a `PARTIAL`, not a clean `NOT FOUND`.

## 6. Adjudication (c): occlusion and two-layer unmixing

**MEASURED (primary-source answer).** Prior art exists for structural layered
restoration. Hasinoff and Kutulakos estimate radiance, layer segmentation,
blur, and ordering and synthesize defocused occlusion with alpha compositing.
Their arbitrary-layer model subsumes a two-layer foreground/background case.
[Hasinoff-2007, §§4–8]

**MEASURED (primary-source distinction).** Bhasin and Chaudhuri do not unmix
two radiance layers. They detect a depth discontinuity and truncate the blur
kernel where finite-aperture rays are blocked, then rerun MAP depth/image
estimation. [Bhasin-2001, §§3–5]

**ARGUED (M79).** The broad claim "nobody has solved defocused occlusion by
unmixing" is false. The narrow claim "no shipping focus stacker is known to do
local layered unmixing from a focus sweep" survived this search, but remains a
`NOT FOUND`, not a universal negative: the major commercial stackers are
closed and were not source-auditable.

**ARGUED (M79).** A microscopy fluorescence volume is not counter-prior-art.
It separates additive emitters in z but has no opaque foreground visibility
term. Conversely, Hasinoff's DSLR result is the right structural community but
the wrong acquisition axis: aperture changes at fixed focus, not focus changes
at fixed aperture.

**ARGUED (M79 answer).** A `strehl` two-layer edge solve could still be novel as
a **shipping focus-stacker implementation**, but not as a model or algorithmic
idea. Its decisive test must compare:

1. one-layer edge plus measured side-lobe PSF;
2. two-layer radiance plus visibility;
3. boundary-moving/seam baseline;
4. held-out-frame likelihood and halo error.

**ARGUED (M79).** If arm 2 cannot beat arm 1 on held-out frames, the apparent
second layer is the M63 side-lobe mechanism and should not be unmixed.

## 7. Adjudication (d): calibrated sensor-noise weighting

**MEASURED (primary-source answer).** Poisson/read-noise-aware inverse imaging
is established. AIDA explicitly weights its data term from read and photon
variance; Ikoma et al. fit gain and read variance from the stack and use a
shifted-Poisson likelihood; Broxton et al. use a Poisson likelihood for
light-field deconvolution. [Hom-2007, §§2.A, 3.C; Ikoma-2018, “Image
formation and noise model”; Broxton-2013, §§3.3–3.4]

**MEASURED (boundary of that prior art).** AIDA assumes flat-fielding and does
not carry PRNU into the likelihood. Ikoma et al. use extensive dark/flat
preprocessing, then assume uniform gain; they explicitly name pixel-dependent
CMOS noise as future work. Their parameters are estimated on the fly rather
than supplied by a separately validated photon-transfer calibration.
[Hom-2007, §3.C; Ikoma-2018, Discussion and Methods]

**MEASURED (thin-surface EDOF boundary).** Forster uses contrast and optional
denoising; Aguet uses white-Gaussian least squares. Neither paper uses
calibrated heteroscedastic sensor variance to decide fusion weights or emit
confidence. [Forster-2004; Aguet-2008]

**ARGUED (M79 answer).** The exact `strehl` claim is **PARTIAL** prior art:
sensor-likelihood-driven restoration exists, including Poisson plus read noise,
but no obtained and read source combines external photon-transfer, read-noise,
and PRNU calibration with a CFA-domain surface EDOF merge and propagated
per-pixel confidence. The inaccessible Kim et al. Bayer-domain paper keeps
this boundary narrower than “no Bayer EDOF restoration exists.”

## 11. Final decision

**ARGUED (M79).** Thirty years of adjacent work say two things at once:

1. **Model-based estimation is real prior art and is worth doing.** Aguet
   already replaced selection with joint latent texture/topography estimation;
   layered computational photography already replaced boundary movement with
   an occlusion-aware radiance model; fluorescence deconvolution already uses
   sensor likelihoods.
2. **The model earns the inverse.** The strongest microscopy results rely on
   controlled z, known or constrained PSFs, additive emission, calibrated
   optics, and usually monochrome sampling. Where those conditions weaken,
   the literature moves to myopic constraints, depth-varying PSFs, heavy
   regularisation, or optical redesign.

**ARGUED (M79).** This rig currently owns the noise model but not the PSF or z
calibration. Its direct measurement says admitted spectra are nearly scaled
copies and that gate width captures most of the available gain. The literature
therefore independently supports M70's verdict:

> **Keep deconvolution as a measured, opt-in plugin. Keep estimation—not
> selection—as the core, but make that estimator a noise-calibrated,
> range-safe surface merge until the forward model earns anything stronger.**
