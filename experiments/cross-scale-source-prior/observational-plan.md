# Separate complete-chip observation after general rejection

Pre-execution decision. Root changed the research question after reading the
failed generated screen. This is a new, explicitly observational experiment;
it does not pass, replace, reinterpret or relax the original qualification.
The original `photographic_replay_admitted=false` remains binding for that
screen (SHA256 a63516111176f8b68a2f3d433ca596f672ea0a02823f71e298c92be546ee9144).

The reason to inspect one photograph is limited: contact interior/edge RGB
errors improved in the generated single-depth contact, while the proposed
bright-outside-spill positive was unexercised and missed its benefit bar.
The same rule caused real glint damage and reduced genuine far/coarse recovery
from about .77–.78 to .22 (roughly72% loss), despite improving near/fine detail.
Flat and genuine radial controls do not erase this failure. An opaque-looking
chip is not an automatic exemption: one opaque scene still contains different
depths, fine/coarse structures and genuine colours whose evidence can disagree.

Exactly one unchanged coarse-prior recipe and one complete299-source chip pair
are authorized. No donor coordinates, mask, radius, exponent, support threshold,
weight formula or postprocessing may be selected from the photograph. Current
and candidate use the held warp/reference149, crop[141,97,3234,2150] quads,
normal optical-CA composition, original u16 sources, same actual rounded gather,
injected model/probe/beta, canonical chunks and held development/tone. The
Current RGB16 pixel hash must equal
`7df99f8f54339711c189380cca45cd63fd8106a2a196162bf562ecc60fbfd7db`.
Any structural/replay/baseline mismatch refuses the observation before a
photographic conclusion. The numerical author does not judge their output.

Review both complete images first, with complete TIFF masters and matching
whole-image views retained regardless of outcome. Then review all seven
previously frozen native regions, in the same output gauge and without local
registration, rescaling, colour normalization or a new sharpness score:

- rail-crossing [1082,942,384,384]
- upper-colour [1020,180,512,512]
- central-stipple [2960,1290,512,512]

- lower-colour [4650,3000,512,512]
- surface-scratches [4840,1240,512,512]
- upper-left-contact-grid [32,320,512,512]

The balanced question is whether broad contacts/compartments gain visibly useful
separation while genuine colours, rails, scratches, thin lines and interior
patterns survive. A smoother or darker region is not itself an improvement.
Lost real colours/lines, widespread damage, or no meaningful contact gain fails
photographic utility. Native regions must be interpreted with the complete
field; an attractive isolated crop cannot override visible damage elsewhere.
No calibrated flat-noise claim comes from these photographic windows.

The retained Helicon whole-field gap motivates the question but supplies no
pixelwise truth: native vendor registration was refused and its input colour,
alignment and tone order differ. Even a useful chip result would license only
further work on an optional mode with explicit tradeoffs. It would establish
neither general eligibility nor SOTA, and would not identify Helicon's mechanism.
