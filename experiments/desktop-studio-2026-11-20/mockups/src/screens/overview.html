Strehl Studio 2: one spine from the rig to the finished file

Strehl began as a focus stacker and grew a capture studio, a diagnostics theatre, an editor and an exporter, each added as another pane beside the last. This proposal re-forms them around the two things they share: the life of one photograph, and the depth that a stack hides inside it.

These are interactive mockups, drawn with the owner's own captures. Pick a screen in the bar above, or use the arrow keys. Numbered marks on each screen are explained beneath it; amber marks reorganise what already exists, blue marks are new capabilities. The written proposal with the evidence, the engineering map and the phased plan is docs/proposals/2026-11-desktop-studio-2.md.

What changed in this iteration

Look

Engraved, not rounded

Square corners, hairlines, tick scales and DIN lettering, taken from the objects on the bench: a lens barrel, a focusing rail, a graticule. No pills, no glass, no cards.

Restored

The plane and its region

Hover any plane on the rail or filmstrip: the photograph shows where that plane was kept, and the plane appears with the same outline. Click to hold the two side by side.

Restored

The region and its sources

Hover the photograph: a region is marked, and its core sample opens beside it with the final crop, the leading planes and a strip per detail size.

Restored

The bar chart

The rail is one bar per plane with a dashed line for the region under the pointer, as in today's timeline, set on an engraved scale.

The life of a photograph

SubjectA specimen or object, with its name, notes, optics and scale.
StackOne sweep through focus: planes, each one RAW or a four-shot group.
RenderOne run of the stacker with a recipe. Renders are kept side by side.
VersionA developed look of a render, with its layers.
ExportA file written from a version, by a preset.

Five stations, in the order the work happens

LibraryWhat you have and what needs you.
CapturePlan, shoot, watch the proof arrive.
StackThe photograph, its checks, its recipe and renders.
DevelopLooks, light, colour, repairs, finishing.
ExportPresets, batches, history.

Nine ideas, each with a screen

1 · Signature

The depth rail

Depth is what every part of the app is about. Today's timeline charts it well, at the foot of one view. One instrument carries it everywhere, one bar per plane on an engraved scale: the lens and bounds when planning, planes filling in while shooting, what each plane holds when checking, the planes to take from when repairing.

2 · Signature

Lenses and the core sample

Evidence is painted on the photograph. Hover a plane and the picture shows where it was kept; hover the picture and a core sample of that region opens beside it, saying in a sentence where its detail came from.

3

Checks in plain words

A count and a list of measured facts, each with its evidence one click away and an action where one exists. No blended score.

4

Flight plan and pre-flight

Capture becomes four numbered steps and a strip that names whatever is still missing. Step size is chosen from a table of its consequences in planes, minutes and gigabytes.

5 · Signature

The proof

The photograph comes into focus on screen while it is being shot, assembled from the previews inside the arriving RAWs. Mistakes show in the first minute, not after two hours.

6

Glance mode and one job tray

Every long task (capture, transfer, checks, stack, repair, export, compact, calibrate) has the same five states and lives in one tray. A full-screen view is readable from the doorway.

7

Subjects with a lifecycle

The library is organised by what was photographed. Each stack shows its life on a line, and the lineage from render to version to exported file.

8

Depth brush and macro finishing

Repair an area from other planes by painting it, with an instant preview. Add healing, backdrop clean-up, crop, white balance picking and a scale bar.

9

Presets and batches

One photograph leaves as several files. Presets, a batch button, resizing, and a history that says which version made each file.
