1Lenses, not panels. Plane, depth, soft spots and alignment are painted on the photograph itself, with one switch. Today the photograph gets 8 % of the window and the evidence sits in nine separate panels.
2Core sample. Today's "This region / source support" popover, kept and tidied: the marked region, the final crop beside the planes that lead there, and a strip per detail size showing which planes were kept. A sentence says what it means. Hover reads live; a click pins it.
3Depth rail and filmstrip. One bar per plane, as in today's timeline, on an engraved scale. The dashed line is the region under the pointer. Hover a plane: the photograph shows where it was kept, and the plane appears above the rail with the same outline. Click: the two are held side by side, with one small map per detail size.
4Checks in plain words. No blended score: a count, then one measured fact per line, each with "Show". "Refused frames", "coverage" and "fallback" are still here, under names a photographer uses.
5Decide while the subject is mounted. The page knows the rig is still on and offers to extend or reshoot before it powers down. The engine's continuation folders already allow a stack to be extended.
6Empty planes become savings. A finding turns into two actions: tighter bounds for the next shot, and an explicit, confirmed trim of the sources already on disk.
7Stations. Library, then Capture, Stack, Develop and Export: the life of one stack, in order, each with its state. The millimetre rule under the photograph comes from the lens data.
