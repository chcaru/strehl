Visual language

Engraved

Strehl is an instrument, and its interface takes its manners from the instruments around it: the white and amber scales engraved on a lens barrel, the vernier of a focusing rail, the graticule in an eyepiece. Flat matte surfaces. Hairlines and ticks. Lettering in the DIN tradition. Colour only where it measures something.

The first iteration looked like any rounded dark application. This one is meant to be recognisable from a thumbnail. The components below are live and follow the theme switch at the top right.

Eight rules

1

The photograph is the page

On every station the picture is the largest thing, on a neutral surround, with a millimetre rule beneath it when the scale is known.

2

Square, ruled, flat

No rounded cards, pills, glows or glass. Structure comes from hairlines, alignment and space. Corners are square; controls carry a one-pixel outline.

3

Scales, not gauges

Quantities are drawn the way instruments draw them: bars on a ruled axis, ticks, an index line. The depth rail, the sliders and the glance dial share one grammar.

4

Colour measures

Amber marks what is selected and the main action. Sage is detail kept. Teal is the plane under the pointer. Status has shapes as well as colours: fine, look, failed, not yet, running.

5

Plain words first

Every reading has a name a photographer would use and a sentence saying what it means. The number and the engine's term follow it.

6

Nothing waits in silence

A task that can take more than two seconds says what it is doing, how far it is, what it waits for, how to stop it and what follows.

7

A disabled control says why

Beside the control, in words, with the fix one click away. Never only in a hover.

8

One word for one thing

Plane, stack, render, version, export. The table at the foot of this page retires the synonyms.

The detail spectrum

Detail size runs from 2 px texture to 256 px shapes. It gets one ramp, cool for fine and warm for broad, used wherever size is shown: the core sample's strips, the marks under each filmstrip frame, the detail-size switch, the engine room's grid.
