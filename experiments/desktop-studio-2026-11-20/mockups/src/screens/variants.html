Three shells, three themes

The screens in this mockup use shell A. B is the smallest step from today; C is the most immersive. They are not exclusive: the recommendation is A as the shell, C as its focus mode, and B as the first release on the way.

A · Stations · recommended

The life of a stack across the top

Library, then Capture, Stack, Develop, Export in order, each showing its state for the open stack. Photograph in the middle, inspector on the right, depth rail below. The left rail and its sidebar go; their width becomes the one inspector.

For: the order of work is the navigation; state is visible from anywhere; one inspector to learn. Against: the largest rearrangement; every pane moves.

B · Rail kept · smallest change

Today's rail, put in order

The left rail stays but holds only the five stations; Agent, Review and Activity move to the job tray and the checks. The sidebar stays on the left as the inspector. The centre becomes photograph first, with the instruments behind one key.

For: reuses the existing pane code and muscle memory; can ship first. Against: state per station needs a second place; the inspector stays on the reading side of the picture.

C · Darkroom · focus mode

Only the photograph, panels as glass

The photograph fills the window. Stations shrink to a pill, the inspector and rail float as translucent panels and fade after two seconds without the pointer. One key (F) from any station.

For: the best way to judge a picture; ideal on one large screen. Against: panels over the image interfere with colour and edge judgement, so it suits looking more than adjusting.

Parts of the shell that are the same everywhere

Open these from the top bar of any application screen in this mockup.

Rig chip

The rig is always one click away

Camera, light, link speed, camera host and receiver in one popover, with the power switches. Turning the light off no longer means going to Capture and scrolling. The chip turns amber for a slow USB link or a stopped receiver.

Job tray

Every long task, one list

Capture, transfer, checks, stacking, repairs, exports, compaction and calibration share one row design: what it is doing, how far, how long, pause or cancel, and what follows. Stacks can queue. "Quiet" holds heavy work while the PC is in use for something else.

Ctrl K

Go to anything, do anything

Subjects, stacks and every command by name, with its shortcut shown. It doubles as the place to discover what the app can do.

Themes

Switch with the buttons at the top right. All three share geometry, type and components; only the colour tokens change.

Graphite · recommended

Neutral around the picture

Today's surfaces are blue-tinted (#10141C), and a tinted surround shifts how colour in the photograph is judged. Graphite keeps today's amber, sage green and warm white on neutral greys, with the surround adjustable from black to white.

Instrument

Today's navy, carried over

The same language on the surfaces the application has now, for anyone who prefers them. It gains a red for failures; today amber serves for warnings, errors and running work alike.

Lightbox

For a bright room

A paper-white theme for working at the rig with the room lights on. The photograph still sits on a dark neutral surround.
