THE CLAIM UNDER TEST
--------------------
ORCH, having looked at the render, reports irregular flat polygonal patches
with brightness steps between them across the defocused background, and
hypothesises (explicitly ARGUED, not measured):

  1. in a region defocused in EVERY frame the through-focus response is flat,
     so pass one's argmax is decided by noise;
  2. the spatial regulariser snaps that random field into piecewise-constant
     regions, i.e. polygons;
  3. each polygon gathers from a different frame, and frames differ in
     background brightness, so polygon boundaries become brightness steps.

I am not accepting this. I am testing it. Note that M116 section 5b already
measured half of it independently and BEFORE ORCH raised it: P17 established
flat-region argmax indeterminacy (outlier pixels sit at structure ratio 0.443
on bee and 0.487 on tweezers; foam, which has no flat region, reads 20.1%
against a 20.0% chance rate), and P18 recorded that a residual mottle survives
the shipped gate. Campaign 5 is the first test of whether anything FIXES it.

Why the soft gate is the candidate, from the source at the pinned SHA
(merge.rs:331-484, read directly, not from --help, per discipline 808):

  * STREHL_SOFT_GATE names a kernel. "gaussian" and "exponential" are the only
    accepted values. unset / "" / "0" / "off" / "false" -> Ok(None), the
    shipped hard gate. "1" / "true" / "on" -> Err, and Estimator::new PANICS.
    A truthy value would therefore not have measured the feature; it would
    have aborted the run. This is the same refuse-to-guess design as
    optics::profile_from_setting.
  * The kernel weights each frame by its response gap from the elected winner.
    Where every frame is nearly equally defocused the gap is small for all of
    them, so all weights approach 1 and the estimator becomes a BROAD average.
    Where one frame is decisively sharper the others carry a large gap and are
    suppressed, so admission stays NARROW.
  * Both kernels share Gate::tolerance exactly (merge.rs:339-342), so only the
    SHAPE of the weight varies, not its scale.

PREDICTED MECHANISM, registered so it can be wrong: the soft gate does NOT
change which frame wins pass one. It changes how many frames pass two averages.
So it should leave the ELECTION polygons intact while removing their VISIBLE
brightness steps, because every polygon ends up averaging nearly the same broad
set of frames. If the election-map polygon count is unchanged between A0 and A2
while the brightness patchwork falls, that is the mechanism confirmed. If the
polygon count itself changes, my reading of the source is wrong.


THE METRIC, AND THE CONFOUND IT IS CHOSEN TO DEFEAT
----------------------------------------------------
PRIMARY: K = excess kurtosis of the Laplacian over a fixed background ROI,
computed on LINEAR renders (never --srgb, which applies a non-diagonal colour
matrix and a guided demosaic and whose own --help says "For looking at, NOT for
measuring").

Why kurtosis and not gradient energy or step count. The soft gate averages more
frames, so it reduces noise by roughly sqrt(n_eff). ANY metric that is a
magnitude of local difference therefore falls under the treatment WHETHER OR
NOT a single polygon is removed. That is the same confound class as the
halo/blur problem of section 5e, and it would hand me a large fake win.

Excess kurtosis is SCALE-INVARIANT: multiplying the field by any constant
leaves it unchanged. So uniform noise reduction cannot move it. What moves it
is a change in the SHAPE of the Laplacian distribution:

  * pure Gaussian noise            -> Laplacian is Gaussian -> excess kurt ~ 0
  * piecewise-constant + noise     -> near-zero inside patches, spikes at the
                                      boundaries -> heavy tails -> kurt >> 0

Direction: LOWER K = less patchwork. A treatment that merely blurs or merely
denoises must read ~0 change. That is the point of choosing it.

SUPPORTING, all descriptive unless a bar is stated:
  E = number of connected components of constant elected index in the election
      map over the background ROI (polygon count, direct from A0-index.tif).
  Neff_bg, Neff_detail = median effective frame count in each ROI.
  D = log10(highpass ON / highpass OFF) on oriented content, coherence >= 0.6,
      worst of R/B/luminance. IDENTICAL instrument to campaign 1's and campaign
      4's CB6/CD7, deliberately, so the cost is comparable across the milestone.


THE ROI, AND WHY ITS SELECTION CANNOT FAVOUR AN ARM
-----------------------------------------------------
The background ROI is selected as the largest connected region whose COARSE
gradient energy (Gaussian derivative, sigma = 32 binned px) is in the lowest
decile of the frame, excluding a 64 px frame margin, and at least 256x256.

sigma = 32 measures "is this region defocused", which is a property of the
SCENE. It is deliberately far coarser than the patchwork being scored, so the
selection statistic is nearly invariant across arms.

REGISTERED CHECK, not an assumption: the ROI is computed INDEPENDENTLY from
every compared arm (A0, A1, A2, A3, A4, M0) and the resulting rectangles must
be IDENTICAL. If they are not, selection is arm-dependent, I will say so
loudly, and I will fall back to the ROI selected on M0 -- which is the arm the
treatment is expected to be FURTHEST from, so the fallback is the conservative
one and it is named here in advance rather than chosen later.

The detail ROI for D is the established oriented-content mask (coherence
>= 0.6), unchanged from campaigns 1 and 4.

G7  MANUFACTURING CONTROL.  ORCH's, and it is the strongest thing here.
    In a region where every input frame is smooth, the merge CANNOT

G8  THE OTHER HALF OF ORCH'S MECHANISM.  Descriptive.
    Step (3) of the hypothesis is that frames differ in BACKGROUND BRIGHTNESS.

    Registered prediction: A1 reduces K by LESS than A2 does, because A1
    corrects the step heights but leaves the random election that creates the

    Registered prediction: E(A2) ~ E(A0). The soft gate acts in pass two and

  AND G5 PASS                       (it reduces the patchwork)

Note the asymmetry with campaign 4 deliberately: G1 is registered as a bar on
ONE subject, bee-3-cpl, and the promotion rule quantifies over the subjects
scored for G6 only. This is the D-t repair from section 5g applied
prospectively: the plant is an ADMISSION GATE for the instrument, not a bar
that every subject must satisfy, and I am not repeating the CD1 error of making
a universal quantifier out of a calibration check.

If G5 passes and G6 fails, the verdict is a TRADE, not a win, and I will report
the exchange rate rather than recommending anything.


WHAT WOULD MAKE ME WRONG
------------------------
- G1 fails: the metric cannot see the artefact even at gate 0, where it is
  known to be worst. No instrument, campaign ends, and that is publishable.
- K falls on A2 but the crops still show polygons: the metric is measuring
  something other than the artefact ORCH saw. The IMAGES adjudicate, not the
  number. I will show the crops either way.
- A1 alone removes the patchwork (G8): the mechanism is photometric drift, not
  election indeterminacy, and ORCH's proposed fix is aimed at the wrong stage.
- G7 shows merged HF <= single-frame HF: the merge is not manufacturing
  structure and the polygons must come from somewhere else entirely.
- E changes a lot between A0 and A2 (G9): I have misread merge.rs and the soft
  gate is not a pass-two-only change.
- The soft gate reduces K but visibly softens the bee's hairs at 1:1. The
  owner shoots insects. That would be a repeat of the halo trade and I would
  report it as one.
