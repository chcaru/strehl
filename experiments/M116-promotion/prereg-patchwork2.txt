M116-promotion — CAMPAIGN 5 ADDENDUM (patchwork, replacement metric)
=====================================================================
Written after prereg-patchwork.txt's G1 FAILED and before any number from
the replacement metric exists. prereg-patchwork.txt is NOT amended: its
verdict stands as written and is reported as a failure. This file registers a
SECOND, separate instrument, with its own bars.


0. WHY THERE IS AN ADDENDUM AT ALL
----------------------------------
G1 was a plant: excess Laplacian kurtosis K had to read at least 2.0x higher
on the gate-0 arm M0, where the patchwork is known largest, than on the
shipped arm A0. Measured on bee-3-cpl: K(M0) = 2.911, K(A0) = 26.660. The
bar required >= 53.32. G1 FAILED, and it failed with the sign reversed.

The crop adjudicates the failure and it is unambiguous. images-patch/
bee-3-cpl__roiG1__A0-A1-A2-M0.png shows M0 as textbook patchwork -- irregular
flat polygons with hard brightness steps, their boundaries drawn explicitly in
the Laplacian panel -- while A0 in the same ROI is smooth. So the PLANT IS
VALID AND THE METRIC CANNOT READ IT.

Diagnosis. Excess kurtosis measures how heavy the tails are RELATIVE TO the
distribution's own scale, which is what made it immune to the denoising
confound. That same property makes it blind here. At gate 0 the polygons fill
the ROI: every pixel is inside a patch or on a boundary, so the Laplacian is
broadly distributed and the tails are not heavy relative to that width. K
stays low while lap_sd goes to 467.24 against A0's 32.78, a factor of 14 in
the correct direction. Kurtosis reads SPARSE outliers. The patchwork is DENSE.

Second, independent defect in the same run: K(A0) = 26.660 was not reading
patchwork at all. The registered ROI selector took the lowest coarse-gradient
decile, which on this subject is the pure white out-of-focus background, and
that ROI contains one small localised blob at its right edge. The blob is a
sparse outlier, so it alone produced the heavy tail. The metric's headline
number on the shipped arm was a dust-scale feature.

Both defects were caught by the registered plant. Neither would have been
caught by the primary comparison, which read K(A2)/K(A0) = 0.0013 and would
have been reported as a spectacular win for the soft gate. That number is
hereby VOID.


1. WHAT CHANGED IN MY UNDERSTANDING OF WHERE THE ARTEFACT IS
------------------------------------------------------------
Selecting the ROI as "flattest" was wrong, and the n_eff map says why. On
bee-3-cpl the median n_eff over the frame is 36.00, but in the pure white
background it is 99.00: the shipped hard gate already admits essentially every
frame there. Where every frame is admitted the elected winner is irrelevant,
so a random election CANNOT propagate to the output. The flattest region is
therefore the one region in the frame where the artefact is structurally
impossible.

The artefact instead lives in MID-TONE, PARTIALLY STRUCTURED DEFOCUSED
regions -- defocused limbs and wings -- which carry enough local contrast to
narrow the gate but not enough real detail for the election to mean anything.
13.39 % of the frame has n_eff < 2.

Consequence for ORCH's hypothesis, which I will report either way: its first
half is confirmed and its second half is refuted where it was aimed. The
election IS a random patchwork in the flat background -- directly visible in
maps/bee-3-cpl__A0-index.tif -- but it does not reach the shipped output
there, because n_eff = 99.


2. ROI SELECTION, AND ITS HONEST STATUS
---------------------------------------
Two named ROIs, fixed here by coordinate, both on bee-3-cpl:

  ROI-W  y=256  x=768   384x384   defocused wing, narrow gate
  ROI-N  y=1024 x=2560  256x256   defocused, median n_eff(A0) = 2.00

DISCLOSURE, stated before results: these ROIs are NOT blind to the artefact. I
found them by eye, from the n_eff map and a contact sheet, and I have already
seen an A0/A2/M0 crop of ROI-W. They ARE blind to the replacement metric,
which did not exist when they were chosen. Anyone reading a per-ROI number
should treat it as a case study, not as evidence of prevalence.

Prevalence is carried by a third, mechanical set, chosen with no eye at all:

  ROI-AUTO  every non-overlapping 256x256 tile whose coarse contrast is below
            the frame median AND whose median n_eff(A0) is below 10.

ROI-AUTO's selection depends only on A0 and on the n_eff map. Both are
properties of the untreated arm, so the selection cannot favour the treatment.
The headline claim of this addendum must rest on ROI-AUTO. If ROI-AUTO
disagrees with ROI-W, ROI-AUTO wins and I say so.


3. THE REPLACEMENT METRIC
-------------------------
The artefact is COHERENT CONTOUR STRUCTURE in a region whose scene content
cannot supply it. The confound is that the soft gate averages more frames and
therefore denoises, so any plain magnitude metric -- lap_sd, lap p99.5 --
falls under the treatment whether or not a single contour is removed.

  F4  =  fraction of coarse-scale gradient-magnitude pixels that exceed
         4.0x the MEDIAN gradient magnitude of the same ROI.

  Coarse scale = 4x box bin before the gradient, which removes per-pixel noise
  while preserving contours several binned pixels long.

F4 is a ratio of the ROI's own gradient distribution to itself, so multiplying
every gradient by a constant leaves it unchanged. Uniform denoising is
approximately such a multiplication.

F4's residual bias is stated in advance and it runs AGAINST the soft gate.
Removing noise lowers the median more than it lowers a contour, so the
threshold falls and F4 tends to RISE on a denoised field. A soft-gate arm that
still wins on F4 wins despite the instrument leaning the other way.

Secondary, reported alongside and never alone:
  L995 = 99.5th percentile of |Laplacian| at full resolution. Magnitude-based
         and therefore confounded by denoising BY CONSTRUCTION. It is reported
         only so the confound's size can be seen, never as evidence.


4. BARS. Registered now, decided before any F4 value exists.
------------------------------------------------------------
H1  PLANT. DECIDING. The metric must read the artefact where the crop proves
    it is largest.
      PASS = F4(M0) >= 1.5 * F4(A0) on ROI-W and on ROI-N.
    If H1 fails, the replacement metric is dead too, campaign 5 reports "no
    working instrument", and NO soft-gate recommendation is made. I will not
    write a third prereg for this artefact tonight.

H2  NULL. F4(N1) - F4(A0) == 0.0 exactly, every ROI. Exact, not approximate:
    N1 is a byte-identical re-render.

H3  PLACEBO. F4(P1) - F4(A0) == 0.0 exactly, every ROI. P1 differs from A0
    only in its private cache directory.

H4  TRAP. F4(T2) - F4(A0) == 0.0 exactly, every ROI. STREHL_SOFT_GATE="0" is
    read at merge.rs:483 as Ok(None), the shipped hard gate, so T2 is a
    source-pinned no-op. A metric that moves here is measuring my harness.
    Note also that STREHL_SOFT_GATE=1 would PANIC (merge.rs:684-687), so a
    truthy value could never have served as the OFF arm.

H5  BLUR CONTROL. DECIDING, and this is the bar the whole addendum turns on.
    The soft gate must beat what pure blur buys for free. Take A0's ROI and
    Gaussian-blur it at sigma bisected so that its L995 MATCHES A2's L995 to
    within 1 %. Blur creates no contours and removes no election error, so any
    F4 improvement it shows is the confound, priced.
      PASS = the soft gate's F4 improvement exceeds the blur's by at least 2x:
             (F4(A0) - F4(A2)) >= 2.0 * (F4(A0) - F4(blur_matched(A0)))
    If blur reproduces most of A2's F4 gain, the soft gate is a denoiser
    wearing a fix's clothes and I report it as such. This mirrors the test
    M87 ran on itself for the halo, which is the strongest form of this
    control in the corpus.

H6  PRIMARY. F4(A2) <= 0.90 * F4(A0), median over ROI-AUTO.

H7  COST. Subject detail must not be averaged away. D = median over the
    detail ROI of the log10 ratio of high-band energy, same instrument as
    CB6/CD7 in campaign 4.
      PASS = D(A2) >= -0.02 dex.
    A2 already measured D_worst = -0.01868 dex on bee under the previous
    scorer, so this bar is live and close: it can fail.

H8  PHOTOMETRIC HALF. ORCH's mechanism blames frame-to-frame background
    brightness drift for the steps. A1 normalises exactly that and nothing
    else. If the steps are drift, A1 alone should reduce F4. Registered
    prediction, no pass/fail attached: I expect A1 to move F4 much less than
    A2, because the contours in the M0 crop are boundaries between DIFFERENT
    SCENE CONTENT, not between different exposures of the same content.

H9  ELECTION INVARIANCE. The soft gate acts in pass two only; it does not
    change which frame wins pass one. So the election polygon count in the
    ROI must be unchanged between A0 and A2. If it moves, my reading of
    merge.rs is wrong and every mechanistic claim here is void.


5. PROMOTION RULE
-----------------
Recommend promoting STREHL_SOFT_GATE only if H1 and H5 both PASS, H2 H3 H4
are all exactly zero, and H6 and H7 both PASS. Any other combination is
reported as a measurement, not a recommendation.

H1 and H5 are admission gates on the INSTRUMENT, evaluated on the ROIs named
in section 2 -- not universal quantifiers over subjects. This is the D-t
repair applied prospectively: campaign 4's CD1 was registered as "on every
subject scored" and became arithmetically unsatisfiable the moment two
subjects failed. I am not repeating that.


6. WHAT WOULD MAKE ME WRONG
---------------------------
- If F4 moves on N1, P1 or T2 at all, everything below H4 is harness noise.
- If blur matched on L995 reproduces most of A2's F4 gain, the soft gate is
  not fixing the election, it is smoothing the output, and the honest
  recommendation is to say so and leave it off.
- If the election polygon count changes between A0 and A2, I have misread
  merge.rs and the mechanism section must be withdrawn.
- If ROI-AUTO shows no effect while ROI-W does, the artefact is rare and a
  default-on recommendation is not supported by two hand-found crops.
- If D(A2) < -0.02 dex the soft gate is buying background smoothness with
  subject detail, which is the trade the owner would notice first.
