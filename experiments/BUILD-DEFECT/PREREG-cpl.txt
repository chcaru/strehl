=====================================================================
2.  WHICH BLOCK IS THE CPL BLOCK -- and the gate on deciding
=====================================================================

A circular polariser costs light (nominally 1.3-2.5 stops).  The two
passes use IDENTICAL shutter speeds, so no compensation was applied, so
the CPL block must be systematically darker at every matched step.

DISCRIMINATOR: for each of the 9 exposure times, ratio of median raw
level (black-subtracted, white-normalised) block2/block1.

GATE C1 -- the labelling is only claimed if BOTH hold:
  (a) the ratio lies on the same side of 1.0 at all 9 of 9 steps, and
  (b) the spread of log2(ratio) across the 9 steps is < 0.5 stop.
If either fails I report the blocks as "1" and "2" and DO NOT claim
which carries the filter.  The main result below does not depend on the
labelling -- only its physical interpretation does.

Moving cloud is the confounder.  Cloud is why (a) and (b) are both
required: drifting illumination over a multi-minute capture will not
hold a constant sign and a tight magnitude across nine separated steps.

=====================================================================
3.  THE PREDICTION -- committed now, from optics, before measuring
=====================================================================

A particle at height z above the photosites casts a shadow blurred by
roughly z / N_eff, where N_eff is the image-side f-number.  The flats are
focused at infinity, so m ~ 0 and N_eff ~ 5.6.

Sensor geometry: A7C II, active 7040x4688 over ~35.9 mm, so 5.10 um per
photosite and 10.2 um per CFA quad.

The mote 2 profile already published (BUILD-DEFECT.md:1465-1474) is a
disc ~30 quads = ~306 um across, and its edge falls from -1.03% to
-3.65% over 5 quads = ~51 um.

    blur ~ 51 um  =>  z ~ 51 um * 5.6 ~ 286 um ~ 0.29 mm

That is the sensor cover glass.  A mote on the CPL -- a FRONT filter,
screwed to the front of a 100 mm macro with a 2x teleconverter, so of
order 150-250 mm from the sensor and sitting where each image point's
beam is wide -- would blur by of order 200000/5.6 = 36 mm.  It would not
be a disc at all; it would be invisible.

    PREDICTION P-A: mote 2 at quad (3434,2305) is present in BOTH
    blocks, at fractional depths agreeing within the block-difference
    null.  It is not on the filter.  I expect NO block effect.

I am predicting a null.  A null is exactly what a broken instrument
returns for free.  Section 5 exists because of that and the result is
void if section 5 does not pass.

    PREDICTION P-B (weaker, genuinely open): mote 1 at quad (3202,119)
    reads only -0.841% on the pooled flats and its open-search blob sits
    22 quads away (BUILD-DEFECT.md Q9).  If mote 1 is block-specific it
    is on the filter and Q9 is answered.  I do not predict which way.

=====================================================================
4.  THE STATISTIC
=====================================================================

Per block b, per site s:
  field_b   = mean over used frames of (quad_mean / frame median)
  resid_b   = field_b / boxblur(field_b, BG_RADIUS)     [the same
              high-pass the shipping census uses: dust is small and
              sharp, illumination is large and slow]
  d_b(s)    = mean of resid_b over the r<=8 quad core, minus 1
              (fraction; negative = darker than local background)

  DELTA(s)  = d_2(s) - d_1(s)          in percentage points

NULL for DELTA: the same DELTA evaluated at every clean site on a
decimated grid, excluding a border of BG_RADIUS and excluding discs
around known sites.  p is the two-sided rank of |DELTA(s)| in that null.

An UNDEFINED outcome is defined and counted separately from a PASS:
a site whose core leaves the grid, or whose null has fewer than 200
members, is UNDEFINED.  Sites are partitioned
      examined = common + block-specific + undefined + underpowered
and the four must sum.  "Underpowered" is its own outcome: if the null
spread exceeds the measured |DELTA| the site decides nothing, and that
is reported as deciding nothing rather than as "common".

=====================================================================
5.  COMMISSIONING -- the result is VOID unless all four pass
=====================================================================

G0  GAUGE / PLACEBO.  Split BLOCK 1 alone into odd and even frames and
    run the identical discriminator.  Those two halves cannot differ by
    filter.  Requires |DELTA| at mote 2 below the 95th percentile of its
    own null, i.e. NOT block-specific.  This is the built-in placebo and
    it is reported whether or not it passes.

G1  PLANTED BLOCK-SPECIFIC DEFECT (commissions RED).  Multiply block 2's
    frames by a synthetic -4% disc of radius 8 at a clean site.  The
    discriminator MUST return block-specific at that site with p below
    the same bar the real sites are judged at.  A discriminator that
    cannot see a defect in one block only cannot report P-A's null.

G2  PLANTED COMMON DEFECT.  Apply the SAME synthetic -4% disc to BOTH
    blocks.  MUST return common, not block-specific.  This is what
    stops G1 being passed by a statistic that calls everything
    block-specific.

G3  PLANTED IMPROVEMENT (required by the project standard; a
    degradation-only suite cannot tell a blind statistic from a
    perverse one).  Take the REAL mote 2 and divide it out of block 2
    only, by dividing block 2's frames by the measured mote profile.
    DELTA must move in the correct direction -- block 2 becomes
    RELATIVELY BRIGHTER at that site, so DELTA must go POSITIVE -- and
    by a magnitude consistent with the removed depth.  A statistic that
    only ever grows under change fails here.

    G3 is the arm I most expect to expose a mistake of mine, because it
    is the only one whose correct answer has a sign.

=====================================================================
6.  THE DISCOVERY ARM, AND ITS FLOOR
=====================================================================

Open search for sites present in one block and not the other -- i.e.
filter dust, which the shipping map does not model at all and which
would matter because 17 of the 26 corpus stacks are CPL captures.

This search MUST be able to return nothing.  Floors, fixed now:
  - |DELTA| >= 1.0 percentage point, AND
  - p <= 0.0025 against the DELTA null, AND
  - the site must be >= SEARCH_BORDER quads from every frame edge
    (the pooled census already showed 37 of its 40 deepest "blobs" were
    edge falloff at a smaller border), AND
  - the deficit must appear in BOTH odd and even halves of the block it
    is present in, agreeing within SPLIT_TOL.

If nothing clears all four, the reported answer is "no filter-specific
defect found at this sensitivity", together with the sensitivity that
statement is made at -- established by G1's planted disc, not asserted.

7.  DECISION RULE, WRITTEN BEFORE THE NUMBERS
=====================================================================

If P-A holds (mote 2 common to both blocks):
    the sensor-space, pre-warp correction site is confirmed against a
    control my prior work flagged and never ran.  No code change.
    The map's framing as a SENSOR defect map is upheld for mote 2.

If P-A fails (mote 2 present in one block only):
    mote 2 is on the CPL.  Then the shipping map is wrong in kind, not
    just in default: a filter mote moves when the filter is rotated or
    removed, so a fixed map coordinate is valid only for captures
    sharing a filter orientation.  I would report that to the
    orchestrator as a correctness bug in the map's premise and NOT
    attempt to fix it in this block.

If the discovery arm finds filter-specific dust:
    report it as a NEW defect class with its measured amplitude; do not
    add it to the sensor map, because it is not sensor-fixed.

Either way the default stays OFF.  Nothing in this experiment is an
argument for changing the default; the default was set by section 13.1
on a different question and this experiment does not bear on it.

=====================================================================
8.  HOW I COULD BE WRONG
=====================================================================

- The two blocks might not be a filter pair at all.  They might be two
  passes with the same filter state (e.g. both with CPL, the "without"
  set being one of the subject-focus-sweep directories instead).  GATE
  C1 is what detects this: no consistent level offset means no filter
  change, and then this experiment measures only repeatability and I
  must say so rather than reporting a filter result.

- The CPL rotates.  If the owner rotated it between the two passes the
  polarisation state of the illumination changes and the two blocks
  differ in overall transmission AND possibly in falloff.  The residual
  high-pass removes smooth falloff, but a strong gradient change could
  leak.  The clean-site DELTA null measures that leak directly, and it
  is what every site is judged against, so leak inflates the null and
  makes me CONSERVATIVE rather than wrong.

- Mote 2 sits 39 quads from the bottom edge, inside the zone where
  BUILD-DEFECT.md:1494 already showed the null annulus reaches into
  optical falloff.  If that inflates the DELTA null too, the site
  becomes underpowered and I must report UNDERPOWERED, not "common".
  Section 4's partition exists so that outcome cannot be read as a pass.

- My optics argument in section 3 assumes the CPL is a front filter.  If
  it is a drop-in or rear filter the distance is smaller and the
  prediction weakens.  Stated so a reader can check the assumption
  rather than inherit it.
