## Findings and required controls

1. **Conditional variance is pre-admission variance.** The affine moments implement the intercept row of the local 3x3 normal matrix and propagate sum(h-squared times injected variance). That arithmetic is internally consistent under fixed weights and original-sample independence. However `within_observed_hull` compares the estimate with the same noisy samples' minimum/maximum before selecting reconstruction versus native fallback. Clipping, focus and motion admission can likewise depend on the realized photons. Therefore this covariance is not the unconditional variance of the delivered accepted/fallback mixture. A flat Monte Carlo that exercises only Moments.solve cannot certify the consumer. Use the actual reconstruct_tile plus coherent-RGB fallback path, separately report acceptance rate/mean/variance, and include a deliberately mixed hull-acceptance case. Author reports an actual-CFA all-accepted flat test exists; mixed acceptance is still required at this checkpoint.

2. **Unknown native variance must not silently remove the affine noise-gain control.** The current excess-variance ratio is checked only when fallback_valid and fallback_variance=Some. With the production native variance unknown, phase Gram conditioning and positive normal-matrix pivots do not bound the sum of squared signed intercept influences. An asymmetric, phase-rich support can extrapolate with substantial negative weights while a broad observed sample range lets the hull guard pass. Compare affine variance to the same-sample local-constant variance, computed from frozen weights without target values; add an explicit bound or retain a measured justified alternative. The author agreed to add this bound and a broad-range asymmetric control after root's compile checkpoint.

3. **Geometry support is not a frequency-transfer certificate.** The second original gather measures the phase Gram using absolute actual affine influence and counts acquisitions with nonzero influence. This closes a no-op-support loophole and is a meaningful necessary geometry check. Absolute influence discards cancellation signs, so neither this Gram nor the positive-design Gram establishes optical detail transfer, alias rejection, or safe nonlinear fallback. Keep the current geometry-only language and use independent analytic/optical and full-photograph controls for benefit.

4. **Shot variance remains a local-constant prediction at edges.** The kernel uses expected_rgb at the output target for all original photosites in its footprint. The frozen guide avoids direct same-target weighting bias, but this is not the true per-sensel shot variance on a steep edge or textured color field. Flat controls validate only the locally constant case. Either evaluate a frozen predictor at each original sample coordinate or retain this approximation explicitly and challenge edge/mismatch sensitivity. Do not call the general propagated quantity exact camera variance.

## Positive source observations

Unique u64 acquisition tokens are checked before reconstruction, with original-byte identity explicitly assigned by the caller. Green's two CFA sites do not count as two acquisitions. Original physical positions and the documented WarpMap width/2 gauge are used, with per-color optical pulls and black subtraction before gain. No aligned image volume is introduced. The affine second pass reuses the same bounded gather and numerical support instead of retaining source samples. Invalid fields and singular pulls refuse; clipped sensels are omitted; unsupported channels expose fallback reasons. Coherent all-RGB replacement remains a consumer obligation, not something inferred from independent per-channel `Reason::Reconstructed` values.

The hull guard avoids the planted ringing counterexample but remains a selection rule. The local-affine option is still opt-in and the default remains local constant. These observations support continued implementation and the specific controls above; they do not establish a photographic sampling gain or permission to skip that outcome.
