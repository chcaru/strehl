## Agreed units and API

Original sensor centers are integer physical `(x,y)` coordinates. The 32x28 generated active rectangle begins at `(104,78)`. The RGGB phase is computed from those original integer coordinates: 0=R, 1=G1, 2=G2, 3=B, mapped to radiance channels `[0,1,1,2]`. A crop does not reset CFA phase. Pixel response is a uniform square `[-0.5,0.5]^2` around each center.

Each view is `[focus, shift_x, shift_y]`. Its reference query is `original_xy + shift + footprint_subpoint`. A layer at depth `d` is queried at that point plus `defocus_scale*(d-focus)*pupil_xy`. Pupil support is the unit disk; `defocus_scale` is native pixels per synthetic depth unit. Depth increases away from the camera, so the smallest depth is tested first. Signed defocus matters to the correlation of intersections on the same ray; a positive blur radius alone is insufficient. This is a supplied paraxial synthetic model, not an estimated optical calibration.

The scene contains two finite capsules: endpoints `[2,2,2]`, positive radii `[2]` in native pixels, depths `[2]`, and three constant RGB radiances `[3,3]` for object 0, object 1, and background. Radiances are camera-linear black-subtracted DN. Background depth exceeds both object depths but is inactive for a constant background. Continuous geometry must be evaluated at each joint sample; there is no raster opacity or independently optimized alpha.

The producer changed its initial Cartesian quadrature **before freeze**: `pupil_xy[K,2]` and `footprint_xy[K,2]` are paired rows of the same joint four-dimensional sample, not a Cartesian product. Default K=2048 fixed unscrambled Sobol points supplies disk coordinates from the first two dimensions and square-footprint coordinates from the last two. This avoids repeating only sixteen footprint positions at exact focus. Every row uses the same pupil and footprint for both layers; visibility is resolved before averaging. Pinhole sets pupil displacement to zero and keeps footprint integration.

`predict` returns f32 RAW DN including black 512; `visibility_weights` returns the three first-hit proportions; `pinhole` returns black-subtracted camera RGB. Source observations are retained as u16 codes. White is 15360. One declared synthetic sensor model is used everywhere: variance in DN squared is `read_variance[phase] + shot[phase]*max(signal_DN,0)`, with read variances `[9,9,9,12]` and shot coefficients `[0.15,0.13,0.13,0.17]`. This is a synthetic authority, not a Sony calibration or a locally fitted variance.

## Independent truth and numerical adequacy

`independent-controls.py` owns the scene coordinates, truth, noise draws, split, and reference. It does not call the producer to create observations. For constant-colour capsules, the independent reference intersects each vertical pupil slice analytically with the finite rectangle and endpoint disks that constitute the capsule. It unions those intervals, clips them to the circular pupil, and computes shared interval overlap before integration. The remaining pupil-x and two footprint dimensions use 8192 fixed shifted Halton3 nodes, with an independent 32768-node selected-query check. This replaces the initially discussed Cartesian Gauss–Legendre source integral before any execution: it avoids concentrating in-focus footprint support into a small fixed grid while keeping the first-run cost bounded. Pinhole truth separately integrates exact vertical footprint intervals using 512-point Gauss–Legendre x quadrature. Both differ from the producer's point-distance predicate and Sobol4 ray sampler. Reference-only f64 quadrature assesses f32 reconstruction precision; observed storage remains u16.

The reference first checks analytic constants, then compares a fixed selected set under the declared standard and denser quadratures. Selected queries include both end caps, the crossing, near/far limbs, every CFA phase, focused and defocused views. Report these differences in DN and supplied sigma before comparing the producer. Reference qualification requires density-discrepancy RMS at most 0.10 supplied sigma and maximum at most 0.50 sigma; the RMS condition assigns at most 1% of sensor-noise variance to this numerical uncertainty. These are declared adequacy targets, not measured error bounds. Producer/reference agreement requires RMS at most 0.35 sigma and maximum at most 2 sigma; do not confuse that finite numerical envelope with calibration or image quality. Wrong-order and marginal-product effects must exceed the larger of four times measured reference RMS discrepancy and 0.02 DN (an f32 accumulation allowance). A numerical ambiguity means the discriminator is unqualified, not a shape-family failure or permission to tune density repeatedly.

Exact constants and deliberately reversed first-hit order commission the mechanism. A marginal-coverage product is also an intentional wrong operator: averaging two coverages before multiplying loses the shared-ray intersection correlation. The equal-colour constant control alone cannot distinguish these operators; the coloured crossing must.

## Fixed small cases, in order

1. **Flat/equal colour first.** The same two capsules and a no-object scene render the same constant camera RGB. Check RAW units, CFA routing, quantization/noise against the supplied model, and final pinhole constancy. Geometry is unidentifiable; a fit must report that fact or use a no-element/constant fallback. A lower training residual is not geometry recovery.
2. **Thin coloured crossing.** A 0.56-pixel-wide finite near capsule and a 0.38-pixel-wide finite far capsule cross between sensel centers. Seven views supply different focus and fractional translations. Evaluate complete 32x28 images, both off-crossing limbs, crossing, both caps, and all CFA phases. Wrong order and product-of-marginals must give a non-null discrepancy supported by independent reference precision. Erasing either ribbon must fail the corresponding truth witness. End caps occur in the independent-density query set, but this first entrypoint does not include a separate infinite-strip replacement discriminator; do not claim that additional negative was exercised.
3. **Known translation observation.** Translate the whole geometry by a fixed subpixel vector and record agreement with the equivalent inverse query translation. This is an ungated finite-sampling observation. The source set changes focus while holding geometry fixed, but no separate symmetric-blur displacement estimator is fitted or commissioned; widening must not be relabeled as a demonstrated geometric displacement.
4. **No-object coloured glint.** Generate a smooth unsaturated background emitter with no opaque capsule. This lies outside the constant-background producer model. A forced two-capsule fit may reduce training loss; it must not be reported as discovered opaque structure. Require a model-domain/held-prediction refusal or explicit unresolved status. Do not call the known no-object generator proof of an automatic no-object detector.
5. **Translucent crossing mismatch.** Near material transmittance is represented in the independent truth by physical alpha 0.35 on each ray, crossing a differently coloured opaque far capsule. Alpha is not fitted by the opaque producer. A single constant-colour translucent ribbon over a constant background is exactly colour/transmittance-ambiguous and cannot establish mismatch detection; the behind-ribbon colour change at the crossing is the actual adverse witness. A low residual alone cannot identify opacity.

The translated and wrong-order cases are cheap forward discriminators, not additional optimizer campaigns. Only the crossing receives the one unknown-geometry fit in this first entrypoint. Flat has a forward/units/noisy-observation control and an explicit identifiability refusal; this is not inverse flat-noise validation. Glint and translucent data are retained as fixed model-mismatch challenges, with a fixed opaque-versus-translucent discrepancy and constant-background-versus-glint discrepancy. Automatic mismatch rejection is explicitly unproved because no additional fits are run. Root can refuse a broader application without expanding model classes.
