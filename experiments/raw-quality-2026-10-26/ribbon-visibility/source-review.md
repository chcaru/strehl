## Agreed first-stage operator

Integer `(x,y)` are original active sensor-pixel centers; RGGB phases R/G1/G2/B map to camera channels 0/1/1/2. For source view `i`, sensor-footprint offset `s` and pupil coordinate `u`, each layer is queried at

`q_l = xy_original + shift_i + s + defocus_scale * (depth_l - focus_i) * u`.

`s` lies in the uniform square `[-0.5,0.5]^2`; `u` lies in the uniform unit disk. The shift is explicitly an original-to-scene translation, not the sign of Strehl's stored reference-to-source pull. Distances, endpoints, widths, shifts and footprint offsets are native-pixel units. Depth/focus are arbitrary shared synthetic units; `defocus_scale` is native-pixel displacement per depth unit. Depth increases away from the camera. There are two finite capsules with positive radii, distinct depth order, constant camera RGB radiances, and a constant background. Background depth has no observable defocus effect while its radiance is constant.

For a given `(u,s)`, evaluate **both** capsule memberships before depth compositing. The front object's contribution is its membership; the back object's contribution is its membership multiplied by front non-membership; background receives the remainder. Average these mutually exclusive contributions only afterward. Sampling independent rays or independently averaged coverages for each layer loses near/far visibility correlation at crossings. This remains true with arbitrarily dense marginal samples.

Capsules use finite point-to-segment distance, with projection clamped at each endpoint and round caps. An infinite-line distance plus a rectangular endpoint cut is a different object. Width below one pixel is geometric occupancy, not material opacity. Endpoints, crossings and cap tangencies must be exercised at several subpixel phases, including after a translation changing the CFA phase.

The agreed `pinhole` result uses `u=0` while retaining the finite sensor footprint and returns black-subtracted camera RGB. This is an all-in-focus finite-pixel image, not a point evaluation of an infinitely resolved radiance surface. `predict` selects the physical CFA channel and adds black 512 in raw DN. Sensor codes remain u16; likelihood and scene calculations are f32. No demosaiced source observations or aligned-frame volume are admitted by this contract.

## Commissioning boundaries

Before source freeze, producer replaced the provisional 128-pupil × 4×4 footprint rule: at exact focus, repeated pupil points cannot repair its 16-point footprint aliasing. The updated contract has **paired** pupil/footprint arrays of equal length, each row one joint 4-D sample. Default is 2048 fixed unscrambled Sobol samples, with the first two coordinates transformed to uniform disk area and the last two to the square footprint. Both layers consume the same row; rows are averaged once, never expanded as a Cartesian product. A 128-query batch consequently contains 262,144 joint samples. This is still a finite numerical approximation, not a proof of adequate integration for a 0.3-pixel hair or near-tangent cap. Independent reference and phase/convergence challenges must determine its tolerance before unknown geometry is scored. A flat DC control alone cannot see endpoint or crossing aliasing. The earlier 64/1024 pupil-adequacy failure is a warning, not permission to borrow a tolerance from another operator.

Hard capsule membership is piecewise constant in parameters under fixed finite quadrature. Ordinary autograd through membership tests supplies no useful boundary gradient. Producer has therefore proposed an explicitly bounded coordinate search, not a fictitious differentiable alpha field. Search changes must still be separated from sampling thresholds: a minimum at one pupil/footprint phase is not geometric evidence until the independent integral agrees. The independent reference must not import or reuse producer membership or quadrature code.

The first inference is at most a small known-two-object problem with three fixed geometry sweeps and up to nine fitted constant colors. It is not automatic topology discovery, full-resolution radiance inference, a calibrated depth estimate, or an actual photograph. Initializing object count, order or geometry from the generating scene must be disclosed separately from fitting unknown coordinates. Known fixed colors would further narrow its claim; fitting colors is more realistic but introduces the ambiguities below.

## Optical and identifiability challenges

- The finite-radius, uniform, circular geometric pupil is not the Sony macro/teleconverter pupil. Nominal f-number, Sony AF fields and the bracketing index do not calibrate effective pupil, magnification, depth, aberration or PSF. A source shift does not include measured focus breathing or field distortion. Those omissions are acceptable for declared synthetic commissioning, not silently transferable to original captures.
- Same-ray geometric visibility models mutual occlusion under its assumptions. It does not make geometric optics exact at subpixel widths: diffraction, wavelength-dependent aberration, fill factor and microlens response can matter. A later common image blur is only a particular approximation. Polarized illumination and iridescent/specular scales can violate fixed view-independent sheet radiance without any motion or opacity change.
- If the two objects and background have equal radiance, every geometry gives the same prediction. The correct result is unidentifiable geometry, not successful geometry recovery. Unresolved width and contrast can also trade; clipping reduces rather than adds radiance information. The unknown fit must not treat a unique returned parameter vector as an identified physical scene.
- A translucent object over constant background has an **exact constant-color ambiguity**. With transmittance `t`, object radiance `C`, background `B`, its covered-ray color is `(1-t)C+tB`. An opaque object assigned that mixed color predicts the same observations. Free-color inference cannot be required to detect translucency in this case. A known-color mismatch check establishes a different, narrower fact. The independent reviewer will use a partially transmitting near capsule crossing a differently colored far capsule to make the background behind it vary, while preserving the constant-background subset as explicitly unidentifiable. This additional condition still does not establish automatic transmittance identification.
- A localized glint is outside the constant-background model. Inserting a short capsule may lower its residual by using shape as a color basis; that is a false structure, not a correct explanation. A no-object foil must retain this adverse interpretation even if training loss improves. A generated glint with changing amplitude may also violate the fixed-radiance assumption.
- Keep one injected sensor-noise model and use original-code likelihood. Flat inverse noise behavior is not established by a forward DC check. If clipping is introduced, count censored observations explicitly. Hidden background detail and unobserved radiance-sheet frequencies remain unknowable; tighter geometry alone does not remove those null modes.

The fixed-visibility prior result is only partially positive: real ridge components improved after radiance refitting, but protected glints undershot by about 3.6–4.7% and a bright held-prediction gate failed. The old free-alpha photograph's CFA-frequency lattice motivates removing unconstrained per-sensel coverage; it does not prove that opaque geometry cures radiance, noise or optical mismatch. Preserve those conclusions even if this tiny operator passes.
