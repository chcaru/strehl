# Independent chip comparator review

VISUALLY REVIEWED: Helicon B provides a substantially cleaner whole-field rendition of several broad chip structures than Current. This is visible as less extended coloured spill and more clearly bounded shapes, not just a general impression of higher contrast. Helicon C does not reproduce that cleanup and shows stronger extended colour contamination in several areas. However, the fixed coordinate matcher refused both external images, so **this run supplies no accepted matched-native detail comparison**. Fine-pattern fidelity, local noise and small-feature superiority remain unresolved.

I reviewed the complete Current field first, then B, then C, and re-read the common `comparison-1` whole-field exports made directly from all three RGB16 masters with identical rounded257 conversion and ICC. These are labelled1617-wide overviews, not native-resolution views. No obvious trial text, logo or repeated watermark is visible in the overviews; this is not an exhaustive full-resolution absence claim. The seven comparison boards contain unavailable external panels by design, rather than silently accepting uncertain coordinates.

The broad, identifiable observations are:

- In the upper-left repeated contact panel, B leaves much less luminous colour spread between the dark contact shapes. Current's neighbouring yellow/green regions look more washed together. C has conspicuous coloured extensions through this area. The overview supports a cleaner separation of these larger shapes in B; it does not establish the correctness of every narrow native gap.
- Around the central group of rectangular chip blocks, B presents more continuous straight boundary segments and clearer steps between the blocks and surrounding substrate. Current retains diffuse multicolour bands crossing those boundaries; C has still stronger coloured extensions and displaced-looking edge bands. Blue/red granular interiors remain visible in B, so this is not a claim that B removes all false colour or resolves their finest pattern.
- The large green field on the right has more contained boundaries in B. C carries long coloured bands and broad contamination near its upper and right edges, with more obvious spread than Current. B also shows a soft dark spot near the right side that is much less conspicuous in Current. Its provenance is unknown from these overviews; it is a difference to retain, not automatically a real feature or an artifact.
- Long scratches remain visible across all three fields. I do not identify a whole-field loss of their main trajectories in B, but neither their narrow branches nor low-level surface grain can be judged reliably at this reduction. B's broader cleanup must not be reported as a measured flat-noise improvement.

Tone and geometry matter. B is generally more crisply separated and locally different in brightness/saturation; C is bright in several contaminated regions. Common PNG conversion and identical ICC remove a display-path discrepancy, but not the pipeline differences: prepared TIFFs are demosaiced/developed before external fusion and carry no prior CA correction, whereas Current gathers with the held optical CA and develops after CFA fusion. Helicon also controls its own alignment and other persisted application settings. Different field extents and magnification remain. Thus neither B's cleanup nor C's spread isolates focus weights, final MHC, CA ordering or a radiometric mechanism. There is no colour standard supporting an absolute colour-accuracy claim.

The B-versus-C contrast is more narrowly controlled: both receive the identical299 prepared TIFFs, ascending order and unchanged application environment. Their very different broad-boundary behaviour therefore cannot be explained solely by a difference in input development or pre-merge tone. The changed merge method, together with any method-specific internal behaviour, is implicated in that contrast. This still does not identify a particular coefficient rule or prove that adopting one part of B would reproduce its result in Current.
