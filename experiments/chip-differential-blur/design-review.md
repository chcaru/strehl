# Independent control choices for the chip differential-blur diagnostic

Preregistered before reading new photographic fit results. This is a test of whether a local displacement is stable to a narrowly specified nuisance-blur model, not a new reconstruction or a camera calibration. No RAW decode is needed: the existing saved, hash-bound CFA patches suffice only after generated controls pass.

Prior evidence must remain intact. The September19 complete299-source/seven-band chip result reported a modest local improvement in existing rail cores and crossings; chromatic stipple remained. The first259-source star-sand transfer was photographically null despite lower held-out guide residuals. This experiment must not reset either result, and lower residual alone is still not a photographic acceptance score.

The independent primary control uses G2 only and a top/bottom spatial split, rechoosing both the fitted green phase and holdout direction. A reciprocal G1/left-right control is required. Real-data reporting must include all four phase/split combinations, not retain whichever has the smallest residual. Fit and holdout source supports must remain disjoint after accounting for guide radius6, Laplacian radius1, maximum displacement4, finite difference.02 and bilinear support1. Source-native displacement units and the actual reference-to-source affine map must be explicit.

The controls use an independently written continuous multi-angle Fourier field, not the implementation's source generator or its discrete Laplacian to construct the expected answer. Exact Gaussian Fourier attenuation produces the known differential blur. Translation(.43,-.57)native pixels, nonidentity scale/rotation, gain1.06/bias.017, and sigma.45 are fixed before execution. Recovery tolerance is.05native pixels. The joint model's expected blur coefficient is negative, approximately minus one half of the source-native blur variance, with absolute tolerance.03pixel². Translation-only beta must be within.03pixel² of zero. These are small-blur model controls, not proof that a Gaussian PSF describes the real lens.

The translation-only, blur-only and mixed plants must actually distinguish their changed terms. Wrong-sign displacement must worsen held-out prediction. The joint model must reduce held-out error over translation-only on the blur/mixed plants. A perfectly flat field and a straight edge must be refused as unidentifiable; an arbitrary numerically converged displacement on either cannot count as support. R/B sample sabotage must leave a selected-green guide unchanged, and the opposite green phase must not leak into it.

One-sided PSF blur is deliberately not classified as geometric motion: its centroid is confounded with translation. An anisotropic PSF is outside the scalar Laplacian nuisance family. Their fitted shifts, if any, are diagnostic failures of interpretation, never ground-truth geometric motion. A control based on one of these must show the ambiguity explicitly; demanding that every misspecified PSF return zero motion would be a false gate.

Real-data admission to any later RAW experiment is narrower than optimization success: all required variants must converge, avoid the4px bound, satisfy local blur magnitude <=1pixel², and hold their displacement within.25native pixels across choices. Substantial beta values (absolute value>.02) must agree in sign. Failure stops before a new RAW render. A stable result can motivate a later bounded complete-image test; it does not automatically pass image quality. Both the old translation-only fit and the jointly refitted fit must be retained. No displacement, blur coefficient, nuisance gain or bias is silently applied to image values here.
