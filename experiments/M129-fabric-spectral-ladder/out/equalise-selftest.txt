(a) does adding variance v raise the floor by v?
       added v  floor before  floor after       rise   rise/v
           500        1636.3       2111.7      475.4    0.951
          2000        1636.3       3591.8     1955.5    0.978
          8000        1636.3       9572.4     7936.1    0.992
    normalisation CONFIRMED: the PSD is scaled
    so that white noise of variance s^2 reads as a flat s^2, which is
    what makes the prescription add_var = scene/target - floor valid.

(b) two copies of one scene, same planted cutoff, different noise
    planted f_c 0.1273 sensor cyc/px (24.9 cy/mm)

                       arm    low SNR    bias %      sd
        quiet, as captured        170     -7.77    1.88
         loud, as captured         19    -17.53    2.51
          quiet, equalised         19    -18.62    1.88
           loud, unchanged         19    -17.53    2.51

    differential bias BEFORE equalising:  9.77 points
    differential bias AFTER  equalising:  1.09 points
    potency: the un-equalised gap is 9.77 points, which must
             exceed 3.0 for the comparison to mean anything: PASS
    the gap shrinks by more than half: PASS
    the equalised SNRs actually match to 10%: PASS

    WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW. The residual gap is not zero and is not
    claimed to be. Equalising the low band equalises the floor relative
    to the texture, but the estimator also sees the roll-off's own shape,
    and two apertures genuinely differ there. On real data that residual
    is entangled with the signal, which is exactly why the native-SNR arm
    is run alongside and the two are compared rather than averaged.
