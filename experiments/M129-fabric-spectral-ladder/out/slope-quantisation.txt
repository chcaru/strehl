  planted f_c                     : 0.127279 sensor cyc/px

  Every measured bias, divided by one bin. If these land on or near
  half-integers, the estimator is returning grid values and the means
  differ only by how many replicates fell in each bin.

  CONFIRMED. The gaps are multiples of one bin divided by the
  replicate count, which is exactly what a quantised estimator
  averaged over replicates produces.

  BUT THE MEAN IS NOT QUANTISED, AND THAT IS WHY THE RESULT STANDS.
  Individual estimates land on the grid, yet the replicate standard
  deviations are 1.05-2.01 points, which is 0.24-0.46 bins, so replicates do
  scatter across adjacent bins and the mean interpolates between them.
    standard error of each row : 0.263-0.503 points
    endpoint difference        : 4.61 points, 10.4 sigma
    a one-row step of ~0.8 pts : about 1.8 sigma -- marginal

  HOW THE SLOPE RESULT MUST BE QUOTED. Quote the fitted slope and the
  endpoints, which are resolved many times over. Do not quote row-to-row
  differences as measurements: the tie between alpha 3.126 and 3.400 is
  the grid, and the non-monotone step at 2.6 to 3.0 is replicate scatter.

  THE NUMBER THIS ACTUALLY PRODUCED FOR THE LANE. One radial bin is
  4.34% of f_c. That is the estimator's intrinsic resolution on
  a single (ROI, aperture) cell, and it applies to the real ladder too,
  not just to this synthetic sweep. Section 2.2 separates H2 from H3 by
  28.07% in the product f_c*N, so the hypotheses are 6.5 bins apart.
  Averaging over the six ROIs divides the scatter by sqrt(6), giving a
  per-aperture standard error near 0.58 points if the real data scatter
  like the synthetic. The separation is comfortably resolvable; it is the
  BIAS, not the resolution, that threatens this lane.
