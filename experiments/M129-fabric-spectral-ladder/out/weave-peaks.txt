  CONTROL. Two earlier versions of this control were wrong and both
  failures were informative. The first compared bins against the
  extrapolated power law and missed every plant. The second demanded the
  detector see a re-emergence of 30% of the floor -- but the detector uses
  the SAME rule as the crossing estimator, so a harmonic it cannot see is
  one the estimator cannot see either, and demanding more sensitivity than
  the estimator has tests the wrong thing.

  The right control is a threshold calibration: plant just above the local
  detection threshold and require a hit, plant just below and require a
  miss. That shows the detector is sharp and that its blind spot is
  exactly the estimator's blind spot.

  IS THE ONE DETECTION REAL? A genuine weave harmonic is a property of the
  fabric, so it sits at a FIXED frequency and must repeat across ROIs and
  across apertures. An isolated bin in a single cell is what chance looks
  like. Both readings are checked.
