  The same six ROIs are measured at every aperture, so the comparison is
  paired and the ROI main effect must be removed before apertures are
  compared. slope_survey.py did not do that, and its verdict is too weak.

  residual sd 0.1730 on 20 df, so the standard error of each

  SIGN TEST, f/18 minus f/11, which assumes nothing about the noise:
    positive in 6 of 6 ROIs, mean difference +0.2198, sd 0.0643
    two-sided p = 0.0312
    VERDICT: the measured slope IS aperture-dependent across the primary
    arm. slope_survey.py's 'aperture-independent' reading was wrong.

  the low band. The predicted steepening uses only the known MTF and the

    The observed +0.2198 lies OUTSIDE the predicted range
    +0.5335 to +0.8450 even allowing +/-0.206 of measurement
    error, so something beyond the MTF differs between apertures in
    the low band. This is a live confound, not a retired one.

  f/5.6 IS THE ODD ONE OUT AND IT IS NOT DIFFRACTION. Diffraction predicts
  f/5.6 to be the SHALLOWEST measured slope, since its cutoff is furthest
  above the band. It is instead the steepest, at 3.402.
  The likely cause is T1, the registered primary threat: f/5.6 has the
  shallowest depth of field, so more of the ROI is out of focus, and
  defocus blur steepens the spectrum. That is consistent with section
  2.3 treating f/5.6 as an out-of-band control rather than a measurement.

  WHAT THIS COSTS AMENDMENT 6. Equalising the low band assumes the band's
  SHAPE is common and only its LEVEL differs. A slope difference of
  0.2198 across a band spanning 2.25x in frequency tilts one
  end of the band relative to the other by 1.195x. The measured
  low-band power ratio being equalised is 1.72x, so the shape
  error is about 27% of the level
  difference being corrected. It is not negligible and it is not
  dominant.
