    # strehl â€” DELIVER-V5
    
    **Start with `crops-1to1/`.** A focus stack is judged at 1:1, and those files
    have no resampling applied at all â€” not a reduction, not a resize, not a
    sharpen. They are integer slices out of the master.
    
    > **The previous gallery could not be trusted at the zoom level it was judged
    > at, and this one was rebuilt because of that.** Everything below states which
    > files are exact, which are reduced, what kernel reduced them, and what was
    > measured on the files that actually ship.
    
    ## What is in here
    
    | directory | what it is | resampled? |
    |---|---|---|
    | `crops-1to1/` | 78 crops, 380 x 380 px, three per subject. Integer slices of the master. A 18 px label bar is appended **below** the image; not one crop pixel is written by the labeller. | **No. Not one pixel.** |
    | `overview/` | 26 whole frames, reduced **exactly 4x** with a **box (area-average)** kernel. `7032 x 4688` â†’ `1758 x 1172`; `7040 x 4688` â†’ `1760 x 1172`. Labelled on the image with the kernel and the measured injected rim. | Yes, and it says so on itself. |
    | `known-defects/` | What is wrong with these files, at 1:1. | No. |
    | `what-stacking-does/` | One frame against the 299-frame master, same scene, matched integer crops. Carried forward unchanged from DELIVER-V4. | No. |
    | `print-masters/` | **The print deliverable.** 26 16-bit TIFFs, one per subject, straight from the stacker. Each carries an embedded ICC profile (`strehl sRGB (IEC 61966-2-1)`) and 300 dpi. No label bar, no reduction. | No. |
    | `manifest.json` | Every file, its master, its crop origin, and its measured rim. | â€” |
    
    **Crops are 380 px on purpose.** An independent audit commissioned the image
    viewer these are read in with a 1 px grating: it resolved at 400 px and had
    collapsed to a flat wash by 600. The previous gallery's crops were 1000 x 1000,
    so every verdict formed on them was formed through a 2.5x reduction nobody
    knew was there. 380 + an 18 px label = 398 px, and the build **asserts** that it
    stays under 400 rather than trusting me to remember.
    
    ## Which file to print from
    
    **Print from `print-masters/`. The PNGs are previews.** The 8-bit previews are
    safe to print from for 49 of the 50 tone regions measured across all 26
    subjects, but `dry-erase-marker-head-cpl` bands across 6.7% of its highlights,
    and the corpus as a whole sits at 0.93x the noise level at which banding
    disappears entirely â€” so 8 bits is clean here only because sensor noise happens
    to dither it, and a subject smoother or cleaner than the ones measured has no
    margin left.
    
    That last clause is the part worth keeping. Banding in a smooth ramp is
    suppressed by noise, this stacker's headline result is that it produces
    **less** noise than the alternatives, and those two facts pull against each
    other: the quieter the render, the less dither there is to hide an 8-bit step.
    The measured floor across 2,391 tiles is sigma = 0.28 codes against a
    zero-banding threshold of 0.30, which is a margin of 7%, not a comfortable one.
    16 bits removes the question rather than managing it.
    
    **Every delivered file now states its own colour space and print size.** All
    185 PNGs and all 26 TIFFs carry the same embedded profile â€” byte-identical, and
    checked by md5 on both sides rather than assumed â€” and a 300 dpi resolution tag.
    An untagged file is interpreted in whatever working space the reader happens to
    have, which is usually sRGB and usually right; these say it rather than relying
    on the guess.
    
    ## Why this gallery was rebuilt: the old one manufactured the defect it was blamed for
    
    The old `_presentation/` gallery reduced the masters ~5x with **Lanczos**.
    Lanczos has negative lobes. A kernel with negative lobes overshoots at a step
    edge by construction â€” it puts a bright rim next to a dark edge that is not in
    the image it reduced.
    
    Reducing the same 26 masters and measuring how far the output leaves the local
    range of the master it came from:
    
    | reduction kernel | negative lobes | injected rim, 26 masters x 3 channels |
    |---|---|---|
    | **box (area average)** | no | **0.000000 DN** |
    | **bilinear** | no | **0.000000 DN** |
    | bicubic | yes | 17.126 DN |
    | **Lanczos** â€” what the old gallery used | yes | **min 1.962, median 13.424, max 36.997 DN** |
    
    **The single loudest complaint about this stacker is halos, and the images that
    complaint was formed on were reduced with a kernel that adds up to 37 DN out of
    255 of bright rim at exactly the edges being looked at.** Some of what was seen
    was manufactured by the resizer and some of it may be real. The old gallery
    cannot tell you which, and that is why it was replaced rather than patched.
    
    Those figures were produced by another lane. They are quoted here because I
    re-derived them from the theorem with my own code and got the same numbers:
    `0.000000 / 0.000000 / 17.1256 / 36.9970`, lanczos median `13.4237`. The one
    figure that differed â€” their min `1.962`, my `3.6714` â€” turned out to be
    definitional, not an error: they publish rim **above** the local range, I
    publish rim **above or below**. On the master that sets it, my bright-rim
    reading is `1.9619`. Two implementations, written independently, agree.
    
    ## What this gallery's own reduction measures
    
    **`0.000000000 DN` injected rim, on all 26 shipped overviews.**
    
    That is not build-time self-reporting. A separate checker re-opens every
    shipped PNG off disk, re-reads the master, strips the label bar, and requires:
    
    - injected rim **exactly** `0.000000000 DN`, and
    - **byte equality** with an independently recomputed box reduction, and
    - every crop **byte-identical** to the integer slice of its master â€” not close,
      equal â€” and
    - that the checker still **fails** when the same files are deliberately
      corrupted. It caught **104 of 104** planted corruptions.
    
    The only residual is `0.500000000 DN`, which is the rounding of an exact area
    average to 8-bit. That bound is arithmetic, not empirical.
    
    `Image.BOX` was **not** assumed to be a box filter. The shipping path computes
    the block mean in numpy; PIL is merely checked against it, and agrees to
    `0.000e+00 DN`.
    
    ## What the rim statistic can and cannot say
    
    It is stated here because a `0.000000` that a reader over-reads is worse than no
    number at all.
    
    **It can** prove that a value in the reduced image is not in the master it came
    from â€” that is the whole theorem, and it needs no edge finder, no plateau
    detection and no site picking, so it has no phantom-site failure mode.
    
    **It cannot** say anything about blur, softness, or lost detail. It was
    commissioned in both directions before use, 36 arms, and two of them exist
    specifically to stop it being quoted as a quality claim:
    
    - **nearest-neighbour** â€” a kernel that physically *cannot* ring, but is a
      catastrophic degradation â€” scores `0.000000000` rim. A second, independent
      axis charges it `182.312 DN`. Without that arm this statistic could have been
      mistaken for one that measures aliasing; another lane made exactly that error
      tonight and convicted the wrong resampler until it added the same row.
    - a master with **half its resolution destroyed** also scores `0.000000000`.
      **A zero here is not a claim that the image is sharp.**
    
    And the overview has a resolution limit that is measured, not assumed: a 187 DN
    disturbance finer than 2 px survives the 4x reduction as **1.000 DN** â€” the
    overview *cannot* show it. The same probe moves **68.062 DN** on a coarser
    disturbance, so that is a resolution limit and not a dead probe. **Judge fine
    detail in `crops-1to1/`, never in `overview/`.**
    
    ## Where the crops are, and why
    
    Not chosen by eye. The frame is split into three vertical thirds and each crop
    lands on the highest mean `|Laplacian|` tile *within its third*, using the crop
    siting code from the lane that owns it. Thirds guarantee separation without me
    picking anything.
    
    That puts the crops where detail exists â€” which is also where a stacking
    failure shows worst. The selection cuts both ways on purpose.
    
    ## What was done to these files
    
    Nothing but 16-bit to 8-bit, rounded to nearest and clipped, plus the box
    reduction in `overview/`. No tone curve, no black point, no saturation, no
    percentile stretch, no sharpening. The masters carry the sRGB transfer curve
    already.
    
    If they look flat, that is the render. The 16-bit masters are in
    `../acceptance/SHIP-V4/`.

    # What the stacking actually does
    
    Two crops of the same place on the same bee, at **true 1:1** â€” integer slices out
    of the full-resolution files, no resizing, no sharpening, no grading. The only
    operation applied to either is a 16-to-8 bit shift, applied identically to both.
    
    | file | what it is |
    |---|---|
    | `ONE-frame-DSC00151.png` | **One frame.** The middle frame of the bracket, 151 of 299. |
    | `STACK-299-frames.png` | **The stack.** All 299 frames merged. |
    
    Measured at that site: mean `|Laplacian|` of **708.7 DN** for the single frame
    against **2385.1 DN** for the stack, a ratio of **3.37x**.
    
    ## How the two crops were made to show the same place
    
    strehl aligns by default, because focus breathing changes magnification frame to
    frame, and alignment therefore shrinks the field. Pixel `(x,y)` in the master is
    *not* the same scene point as `(x,y)` in a single-frame render, so cropping at
    matching coordinates would have compared two different parts of the bee.
    
    The scale and shift were fitted by normalised cross-correlation on 8x-downscaled
    copies: **scale 1.070, correlation 0.9403**. That 7% independently confirms the
    alignment shrink the tool documents.
    
    **The search resampled. The output did not.** The delivered crops are integer
    slices out of the full-resolution files.
    
    ## What this does and does not show
    
    It **does** show the thing focus stacking exists to do. Any single frame has one
    thin plane of focus; at this location frame 151 has none of it, and the stack
    resolves the ommatidia lattice across the full curvature of the eye, near side
    and far side both.
    
    It **does not** show that the stack beats the best available frame *at this
    site*. Two caveats, and they cut the same way:
    
    1. The crop location was chosen where the **master** has the most detail, which
       favours the stack by construction.
    2. Frame 151 is sharp **somewhere** â€” just not here. It sits at its own point in
       the sweep.
    
    The honest hard question is the other one: is the stack at least as sharp as the
    sharpest frame *at each pixel*? That is measured separately and is not answered
    by these two files.
    
    ## Viewing
    
    **At 100%.** These are 380 x 380 so that an image viewer cannot scale them away.
    A viewer showing them scaled-to-fit will make both look soft regardless of what
    is in them â€” which is exactly how the earlier 1400 px gallery misrepresented
    every render it was used to judge.
