## What the projection can and cannot remove

**DERIVED, under one spatially invariant radial PSF and nonzero spectra.** Set `L(u,theta)=log|F(exp(u),theta)|` and `Q=L-mean_theta(L)` at each radius. A similarity plus radial convolution has `L2=c+shift(L1)+log|H(exp(u))|`; angular subtraction removes both additive radial terms and commutes with the log-radius/angle shifts. Correlating Q therefore estimates scale/rotation without fitting a defocus radius or multiplying a biased real-space result by a gain. This is the new discriminating mechanism; it avoids the tile displacement parabola rather than correcting its measured response.

**DERIVED failure stronger than a radial-scene null:** every separable magnitude `|F(r,theta)|=R(r)A(theta)` loses ALL scale information after this projection, even when A is strongly anisotropic. A power-law spectrum with fixed angular anisotropy is one example. Q can be large and structured while constant in log radius. A clean peak or large angular energy must not be accepted as proof of scale information. An informative positive stimulus needs angular structure that changes with radius.

For multiple depths, `G=F1*H1+F2*H2` in Fourier notation is a sum, so the log cannot separate into one radial nuisance. Occluding visibility makes the model still less separable. Windowing is spatial multiplication and therefore Fourier convolution: multiplying by a fixed Hann window does not preserve the identity. Translation invariance of the unwindowed magnitude also does not guarantee invariance after cropping/windowing. Discrete log-polar interpolation, aliasing and finite support can create their own scale bias. Noise or a log floor near an OTF zero breaks exact cancellation; excluding pixels based on the same noisy feature can manufacture the match. Polygonal apertures and aberrations need not be radial. Magnitude has the usual 180-degree ambiguity. These are failure inputs, not reasons to tune a sweep until a peak appears.

## Primary literature and previous project losses

- Reddy and Chatterji, 1996, give the Fourier-magnitude/log-polar scale/rotation construction and its 180-degree ambiguity. Their registration result is not a demonstration of defocus invariance. - Ojansivu's 2009 dissertation describes earlier blur-invariant rotation/scale/translation registration using even powers of phase-only bispectrum slices and log-polar mapping. It is a different descriptor; no first-invention claim is warranted. [Author's university record and abstract](https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/handle/10024/35209). The PDF open failed, so no body-level replication claim is made.
- Flusser et al., 2023, use projection-based Fourier ratios, distinguish circular from polygonal defocus, and warn about small denominators/noisy high frequencies. Their multifocus example explicitly acknowledges that one convolution holds only on a foreground or background; its demonstrated registration is translation only, with qualitative fusion output. It does not establish macro breathing-scale accuracy. Their projector is not this subtraction of log magnitude. [Paper, sections3,5.4–5.5,7.4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-023-01798-7).
