# Direct photosite reconstruction: fixed first falsifier

2026-09-14, written before the joint solver's numerical or photographic results.

## Why this branch

The matched chip source already shows colour breakup through MHC, and several
single-frame alternatives trade that breakup for genuine colour loss. Published
RI improves rail continuity locally but loses twelve eligible synthetic cases
against shipping MHC. Two representative losses persist in untouched upstream
RI, so neither a Rust port nor further RI tuning is commissioned.

The August proposal's C1 consumes actual photosites jointly. The targeted prior
work check found no implemented RAW sample-fusion consumer. PHASE's final
disposition is NOT ROBUST, not an unconditional kill; M193 validates the existing
interpolated consumer. Their old geometry bars do not validate or refute this
different reconstruction. The nine-frame nominal map has usable local phase
diversity, but that is not proof of registration accuracy or common focus.

## Fixed first operator

One native-resolution RGB grid, reference149 sensor coordinates, no 2x output.
Original same-channel CFA observations carry their exact inverse similarity
coordinates; no per-frame MHC enters the fit. Solve independent channel systems
with a bilinear forward operator, provided inverse noise variance weights and
a fixed 0.01 first-difference quadratic prior. Variance comes once from the
shipping ISO100 A7C2 profile and follows black subtraction, first-frame WB and
normalization. The prior is an algorithm parameter, not a fitted noise model.

This deliberately small first operator has a known possible failure: bilinear
forward interpolation attenuates continuous high frequencies and may force
exaggerated reconstructed contrast. Analytic continuous scenes, rather than
observations generated by that same bilinear operator, must expose that bias.
The test compares absolute RGB, green and chroma truth errors, with flat noise
first. Gradient energy alone is inadmissible: added noise or ringing increases it.

The independent reviewer chooses continuous neutral and independent-colour
patterns, diverse and duplicated sample phases, wrong placements and unequal
blur. A sharper-looking result is not accepted if truth error or false colour
rises materially. Duplicate or poorly constrained samples may yield a numerical
solution through the prior; this is not newly measured detail. Data-supported
and prior-filled pixels are reported separately. A blur-mismatched fixture must
expose the common-latent-scene assumption rather than silently count it as truth.

## Actual RAW scope, only after the first synthetic result

The existing chip map uses reference149, active sensor7032x4688. The fixed target
is native sensor(1332,1104),392x392, matching the saved focused-source patch.
Indices145..153 are retained as original u16 CFA. Every retained crop agrees
with an independent rawler decode. LibRaw's visible width is7028 while rawler's
map width is7032; their common crop coordinates are verified, not inferred.
The initial staging attempt refused its incorrect equal-width assertion before
saving a patch, and remains recorded.

Exact16383 flags remain attached to observations. Excluding a pinned sample
does not establish highlight recovery: a defocused donor can merely darken a
highlight. Unsupported sites remain explicit. This first crop experiment has
no per-channel CA correction or depth-dependent PSF, and its source-coordinate
comparison is distinct from the corrected full299-frame merged photograph.

If synthetic controls justify an actual run, inspect a native product-developed
crop at the exact saved tone/matrix. Use a leave-two-frames-out prediction check
(147 and151) against the same held observations predicted from reference149 MHC,
and a wrong-placement control. Lower held-out residual supports consistency;
it can still favour blur and therefore cannot establish photographic quality.
No full-stack reconstruction, default change, SOTA claim or large benchmark
follows from this initial patch test alone.
