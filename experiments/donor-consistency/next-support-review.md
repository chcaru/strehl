# Next support route: saved, not authorized

Read-only decision, 2026-09-19. The corrected-address manual oracle can test
headroom but does not supply an automatic ownership rule. The original failed
oracle and the concentration locator remain closed.

The nearest dark-core concentration track48 is not an isolated point: its
reported component contains3652 pixels at source33 and7950 at69; every frame
touches the fixed measurement disk or display ceiling. Tail track58 has no
positive peak in46 frames, including25/28/33/35, while its late positive
centroids move upstream. A focused source need not have a positive peak at a
defocused tail address: concentrating a glint removes that off-core signal.
The locator's point-peak eligibility therefore cannot establish tail ownership.

An unconstrained positive forward-blur fit is also insufficient. A compact
core approximates a delta, so its fitted kernel can absorb almost any positive
late patch, including a genuine bright feature at another depth. Failure of
the reverse fit does not distinguish these cases. Tail offsets unobserved
elsewhere are underdetermined; fitting them on the target tail is circular.
An independently constrained kernel or another shared feature would be needed
before this could support a physical explanation. Toned RGB cannot calibrate it.

A connected-footprint lineage with mutual overlap could instead propose
support, refusing splits/merges, but is uncommissioned. Its decisive destroyer
is a real thin feature at another depth crossing the spreading glint: that
feature must retain its own ownership or remain unknown. A rule which refuses
every real glint also fails. Neither proposal is licensed for implementation
by the current evidence. Root has deferred both and resumed the previously
positive complete-CFA wavelet candidate's boundary/rounded-gather test.
