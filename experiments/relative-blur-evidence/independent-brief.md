# Independent relative-blur falsification brief

2026-09-19. Design only; no implementation, execution or RAW read. **Authorize
only a small model-adequacy discriminator, not ownership or fusion.** Author's
proposed family is a nonnegative unit-mass, zero-centred elliptical Gaussian,
one symmetric covariance shared by all four CFA planes; identity allowed,
no fitted gain, offset, displacement or channel-specific kernel.

## The decisive fixture

Use one fixed256x256 quad-domain linear four-plane scene, sampled at the
corresponding native CFA sites. Two separated training features at depth A
(an oblique textured edge and a small coloured spot) constrain different
directions/frequencies. A third, held-out compact coloured glint at the same
depth is the positive target. Its broad support crosses a genuine thin ridge
at depth B; give that ridge both achromatic detail and independent red/blue
modulation so green agreement alone cannot certify it. All structure and
evaluation supports come from the independent latent scene, never fitted
residuals or a selected output.

Construct three observations from separately blurred layers: A sharp/B blurred,
A blurred/B sharp, and an intermediate focus. For the in-family positive use
one fixed anisotropic Gaussian with standard deviations6 and2.5 quad pixels,
rotated30 degrees; the spread reaches outside the compact core. Render an
additive transparent-layer crossing first, where component truth is exact.
The ridge must continue through and beyond the spread footprint, so selecting
one source over the whole region visibly destroys valid narrow-depth detail.
Also retain the identical pair with the glint absent: a real ridge must not be
called optical spread merely because smoothing predicts it well.

Fit only the two training features. Freeze their geometry before fitting;
exclude the held-out target and its entire maximum convolution apron from
every training stencil. Preserve a genuine source apron for held-out scoring;
no circular wrap, zero-filled unseen source or refit on the disputed tail.
Evaluate both ordered directions separately on (a) held-out same-depth glint,
(b) crossing ridge and (c) uncontaminated ridge continuation. The mixed-depth
region must remain ambiguous/refused unless the proposal explicitly resolves
its separate supports. A single low aggregate residual must not assign it to A.
Zero focused-source signal at the off-core tail is expected in the positive:
concentrating the glint removes that signal. It is not evidence against A.

## Small fixed counterexamples and decision

- Run a constant/flat pair first, then identical structured frames. Neither
  permits a strict source order. Repeat the positive/crossing fixture with the
  existing injected ISO100 model; include input-source noise as well as target
  noise in any residual allowance. This tests an operator, not sensor calibration.
- Replace only the positive's spread by a centred, normalized nonnegative
  mixture of a compact lobe and two narrow offset lobes, with different RGB
  mixture weights: a coloured specular fan outside the shared-Gaussian family.
  Also test the fixed clipping case with the source core saturated. These must
  not become confident ownership claims; refusal is an informative family limit.
- A gain-only scaled copy with unchanged spatial shape, plus the pair with
  reversed input order, challenges blur-versus-brightness identification and
  accidental preferred-index rules. No gain nuisance may be added after failure.
- Require a correctly directed, nontrivial held-out positive in the unclipped
  in-family case, and preserved/unknown crossing-depth support. Refusing every
  case fails utility; accepting the crossing as one owner fails safety. Record
  actual per-support errors and direction, not just a single fit score. Numerical
  acceptance tolerances must be frozen before implementation is measured.

Passing this screen would justify one bounded diagnostic on already retained
actual linear gathered-source witnesses. It would **not yet justify an actual
fusion consumer**: compatibility on the real compact-core/fan pair and refusal
of its competing-depth structure must first be demonstrated. A synthetic
Gaussian generated by the fitted family can validate fitting, not prove that
real lens defocus belongs to that family. A compact delta can explain almost
any positive patch under an unconstrained kernel, and reverse-fit failure does
not resolve that ambiguity.

Prior limits retained: `donor-consistency/next-support-review.md` identifies
this identifiability problem. M115 §5 rejects deriving PSF scale from focus-curve
width and finds nearly scaled off-focus copies on its tested regions; its
reconstruction null is not a proof that every ordering test fails. M87 §§4–5
shows artifact suppression and model-fit statistics improving while truth or
real fine structure gets worse. Its numeric fit is not a correctness oracle.
This brief therefore judges held-out component truth and mixed-depth refusal,
not sharpness, reverse failure or cosmetic tail suppression alone.
