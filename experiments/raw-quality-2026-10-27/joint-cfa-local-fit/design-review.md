## Required implementation details

1. **Actual metric for the Parseval null.** Set the final coefficient metric `q = a/max(V,1)` to exactly one in this control, not merely `a=1`. Heteroscedastic inverse variance otherwise prevents reduction to ordinary raw squared error. Use the complete Haar transform including every detail, terminal lowpass and odd carry. Report actual levels:128x128 CFA panels give64x64 phase planes and six levels, despite the maximum of seven.
2. **Variance and physical phases.** For an independent pair with variances `v0,v1`, both Haar output marginal variances are `(v0+v1)/2`; an odd low carry retains its variance. Apply the same rule on both axes and recursively only to the lowpass. Low/high covariance is `(v0-v1)/2`, generally nonzero. The design correctly calls the objective a surrogate and does not invert that full covariance. Smoothing squared coefficients and marginal variances with identical normalized clamped-border weights gives a noise-energy expectation, not independent samples. Keep G1/G2 source coordinates and injected phase parameters distinct even though A addresses the same latent G channel.
3. **One weighted linear system.** Freeze weights from original data before solving. Algebraically the operator is `B = sqrt(q/N) H A`, with right-hand side `sqrt(q/N) H y`; its transpose must reverse exactly those operations. Finite weights alone do not protect f32 normal-residual squared norms. Use one common positive scale `s=max(q)` and the metric `q/s` consistently in both directions, the RHS and true residual. Record s and the original objective, with stable f32 norm/objective reductions; this preserves the minimizer and relative normal-residual criterion, not the conditioning. Reject an invalid/all-zero metric before division; exercise high-DN, all-tiny positive metric and zero-residual controls. If the unscaled objective is not representable, report that explicitly rather than claim a finite value. Handle a zero initial normal residual before division, finite/positive curvature, and recurrence failure explicitly. True normal residuals and objective are separate from recursive diagnostics. The `1e-4` admission floor is not a ridge or a rank guarantee: every finite-variance observation remains represented, including blurred disagreement. Preserve numerical refusal rather than silently capping or retuning weights.
4. **Support before any fit.** An eight-pixel apron is compatible only with bounded transforms. For example, a128-square with source-to-reference scale1.12 and rotation.17radians extends approximately19pixels beyond its original half-width before Lanczos and footprint support. Freeze test maps that fit the declared apron, or establish the exact support bound before generating data; do not crop or extend observations after failure. Reusing the old basis arithmetic must not import its unrelated64-pixel blur-radius guard. The output reference-cell operator also needs complete support and its own adjoint/constant check.

## Remaining risks are experimental questions

The least-norm solution from zero is an implicit image choice, not a resolved radiance field. Red/blue sampling ambiguity, free apron degrees of freedom and independent colour weights can create false detail even with a small residual. A geometry-only A cannot predict depth-dependent defocus or occlusion; band admission only changes the penalty assigned to that mismatch. The original-CFA alias-cancellation input and diverse-phase companion remain particularly useful because the proposed source energy can prefer blur. Clipped observations should cause the declared whole-case refusal in this first scope; dropping irregular samples would change the complete Haar/null contract.

Keep the fixed continuous near/far positive, independent colour/flat-green/8DN-green guards and complete-panel output inspection.
