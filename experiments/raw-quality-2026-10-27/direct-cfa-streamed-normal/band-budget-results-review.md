The full-size CPU parity shows that this boundary behavior is shared by the specified transform, rather than evidence of a CUDA transpose bug.

## Bounded next decision

ARGUED proposal only, not implementation release: retain original sensor A and the injected noise, but change the two directly implicated mathematical choices together. An area-aware orthonormal pair uses child support counts n1/n2, with low `(sqrt(n1)*l + sqrt(n2)*r)/sqrt(n1+n2)` and high `(sqrt(n2)*l - sqrt(n1)*r)/sqrt(n1+n2)`. This keeps constants in LL across unequal carries; inverse, marginal variance and support must use the corresponding coefficients. Retain actual measured A(ones) for deflation rather than substituting ideal constants.

For the first discriminator, use the producer's fixed per-level precision-mass normalization: over the same admitted sources, physical phases and orientations at AC level k, let `Qk=sum(a/max(V,1))` and `Rk=sum(1/max(V,1))`, then `q'_k=q_k*(Rk/Qk)`; leave LL unchanged. This removes the measured cross-level total-weight imbalance while exactly preserving within-level source, position, color and orientation preferences. It avoids introducing focus ties through saturation. Entirely unsupported levels remain omitted; positive R with nonpositive Q is a refusal. It is a changed surrogate objective, not preconditioning or a numerical repair. Large within-level glint/color dominance and poor conditioning can remain. On a flat where all admissions equal the floor, normalization restores approximately 1/V, so this is not a denoising guarantee. Existing mixed-depth, independent-color and flat controls remain material; noise and free-color ambiguity are not automatically solved.
