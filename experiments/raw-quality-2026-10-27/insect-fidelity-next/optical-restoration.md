## The precise obstruction

**DERIVED:** even on one stable diffuse patch, relative through-focus blur does not identify the common in-focus blur. If every kernel factors as `h_f = g * k_f`, then observations of `h_f * x` equally describe relative kernels `k_f` applied to object `g * x`. Original-CFA sampling, more frames, and unit-sum kernels do not by themselves choose between those explanations. A sharp-looking frame is not an independently unblurred object. RGB weighting adds another ambiguity when wavelength-dependent blur, colored structure, and illumination are unknown. Occlusion and specular changes can violate the shared-object convolution before this ambiguity even arises.

This is consistent with the primary [Aguet, Van De Ville and Unser paper, section VI-A](https://www.francoisaguet.net/pdf/Aguet08.pdf). Its reflected-light fly example is relevant, but the paper distinguishes robustness of estimated topography from accuracy of the in-focus PSF. The latter affects reconstructed texture and false color. Its 30% parameter-tolerance result comes from model-generated data and concerns topography; it does not license an arbitrary macro-lens kernel. Only the paper was consulted; no restricted EDF implementation was opened.

A correct injected Sony noise model cannot remove this ambiguity. In particular, single-frame variance is not the covariance of a nonlinear merged sensor master, and conditional probe propagation is not proof of the selection noise. A post-merge optical Wiener filter using either as if it were measured merged covariance would reopen the old unsupported assumption.
